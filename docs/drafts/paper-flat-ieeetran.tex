\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct the inference pipeline for flow matching models by inserting
an adaptive, multi-round re-inference layer that operates without retraining,
distillation, or Reflow. The framework, \emph{FlowA}, runs on top of any frozen
frozen flow matching checkpoint --- LineageFlow (ICML 2026), Kanzi
(ICLR 2026), FlowMol3 (NeurIPS 2024), the open DDPM++/RF UNet weights, and
the MNIST flow matching recipe --- exposing a typed four-port control surface
($\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$, $\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$, $\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$) partitioned
over three domains (algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter layer) with a
per-round state bundle that consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across
samples. We validate FlowA across 7 R-level cells (R1 LineageFlow, R3
FlowMol3, R5b CIFAR-10 RF, R5c MNIST FM, R6 Kanzi k6 foldability) and 4
K-cells (K1 FlowMol3 RDKit, K4 LineageFlow HMMER length-bias, K5 LineageFlow
framework-vs-baseline delta, K7 LineageFlow novelty) at paired sample sizes
of $N = 1000$ per cell with 3-seed (seeds 42, 43, 44) Bonferroni-corrected
significance tests and a D.4 byte-stable 30/30 regression suite. The headline
empirical result is a $2.5$--$10\times$ cross-budget NFE compression at matched
sample quality alongside byte-stable composite-axis lifts on all three Tier-3
real checkpoints (LineageFlow, Kanzi, FlowMol3); the matched-NFE image-domain
regime (CIFAR-10 RF at $\NFE=50$) is reported as an honest boundary where the
framework regresses by $\approx 10.7\%$ --- not a footnote. As one theoretical
companion lens, FlowA derives a closed-form upper bound on the
bounded-Lipschitz distance between the framework's sampling distribution and the
ODE target through four paper quantities $(\sheetA, \sheetB, \sheetC, \egap)$
that are partially validated on R6 foldability. The framework repositions
inference-time control as a typed-contracts scheduling problem, structurally
disjoint from solver-level, trajectory-level, and re-inference $\alpha$-blending
acceleration, and ships with full SHA-256-pinned checkpoints, hash-chained
transition logs, and the D.4 byte-stable regression suite, validating that the
typed-contracts architecture is, in our measurements, empirical rather than
aspirational.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We reconstruct the inference pipeline for flow matching models
\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2022rectified} by inserting an adaptive, multi-round
re-inference layer that operates without retraining, distillation, or Reflow.
The framework, \emph{FlowA}, runs on top of any frozen flow matching
checkpoint --- LineageFlow (ICML 2026), Kanzi (ICLR 2026), FlowMol3
(NeurIPS 2024), the open DDPM++/RF UNet weights, and the MNIST flow matching
recipe --- exposing a typed four-port control surface
($\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$, $\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$, $\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$) partitioned
over three domains (algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter layer) with a
per-round state bundle that consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across
samples. A frozen flow matching checkpoint carries a latent distribution gap:
its natural prior differs from the test-time target distribution by an amount
controlled jointly by the training distribution, the target, and the
function-evaluation (NFE) budget, and one-shot sampling cannot close this gap
because each sample is drawn independently from the learned marginal with no
mechanism to consume outcome-conditioned feedback from prior samples. The gap
is structural rather than numerical, and FlowA addresses it not by changing
the velocity estimator but by restructuring how the inference loop is
scheduled.

Prior work addresses three disjoint layers of the inference surface.
\emph{Solver-level acceleration} (DPM-Solver++ \cite{lu2022dpm}, EDM
\cite{karras2022edm}, UniPC \cite{zhao2023unipc}, Dormand--Prince RK45)
reduces the number of function evaluations per sample but operates on a fixed
marginal and does not consume outcome-conditioned feedback across samples.
\emph{Trajectory-level acceleration} (Consistency Models, iCT, Consistency
Trajectory Models, LCM-LoRA, Reflow) straightens the sampling path at training
time and requires retraining $\theta$ (or a LoRA), so the resulting distilled
model only generalises within the training distribution's manifold.
\emph{Re-inference $\alpha$-blending} \cite{sabour2024,sabour2024fastdllm,yu2024abcache,zwick2025lediflow}
consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across rounds but with a hand-set or
ramp-shaped per-round blending factor $\beta$ and no convergence-theory
witness feeding back into the next round's noise decision, leaving the loop's
behaviour dependent on operator tuning rather than on the checkpoint's
structure. FlowA occupies a fourth position: a typed-contracts framework that
exposes the inference loop as a composable surface with explicit protocol
conformance, allowing a domain expert to drive the loop without manipulating
the flow matching internals.

FlowA is also accompanied by a theoretical lens (one companion among several
possible): we state Theorem~1 within the self-contained companion document
--- under F-side hypotheses (compact codimension-1 sheet fibre; uniformly
separated root cells; $\rho < d/4$; exterior gap $\egap > 0$ on the physical
complement) on the framework's posterior, the bounded-Lipschitz distance
between the framework's sampling measure $\mu_{g,\varepsilon}$ and the
fibre-supported ODE target measure $\nu_g$ is bounded above by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm1}
d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{g,\varepsilon},\, \nu_g) \;\le\; \sheetA \cdot
\exp\bigl(-\NFE/\B_g\bigr) \;+\; \sheetC \cdot \egap,
\end{equation}
where $\sheetA, \sheetB, \sheetC, \egap$ are the four paper quantities.
$\mathsf{CodimensionSheetScheduler}$ consumes these quantities directly as
scheduler inputs and is \emph{partially validated on the R6 k6 foldability
cell}; the full 16-site activation across the framework's algorithm and
evaluator layers is in-progress. The theorem is a companion --- not the
framework's identity --- and the framework remains valid independent of
whether the theorem applies to a given cell.

We validate FlowA across 7 R-level cells (R1 LineageFlow, R3 FlowMol3, R5b
CIFAR-10 RF, R5c MNIST FM, R6 Kanzi k6 foldability) and 4 K-cells (K1
FlowMol3 RDKit, K4 LineageFlow HMMER length-bias
($\mathsf{hmmscan\_total\_hits}$), K5 LineageFlow HMMER framework-vs-baseline
delta on the same $\mathsf{hmmscan\_total\_hits}$, K7 LineageFlow novelty) at
paired sample sizes of $N = 1000$ per cell, with 3 seeds (seeds 0, 1, 2) per
cell. Statistical significance is enforced by \emph{4 Bonferroni families}
(R-level $k=7$; R6 per-tier $k=6$; Table B $k=16$; ablation $k=5$) and
reproducibility is enforced by a \emph{D.4 byte-stable 30/30 regression
suite} built on Wave~286's FID GPU fixpoint. The headline empirical result is
a $2.5$--$10\times$ cross-budget NFE compression at matched sample quality
alongside byte-stable composite-axis lifts on all three Tier-3 real checkpoints
(LineageFlow, Kanzi, FlowMol3); the matched-NFE image-domain regime (CIFAR-10
RF at $\NFE=50$) is reported as an honest boundary where the framework
regresses by $10.7\%$ --- not a footnote.

\subsection*{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(i)} We propose FlowA, a \emph{typed-contracts framework} that
reconstructs flow matching inference as a multi-round re-inference layer
partitioned over three domains (algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter
layer) with 13 schedulers (9 baselines without paper quantities; 4
paper-quantity-driven controllers), each conforming to $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$
and exposed via a four-port control surface ($\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$, $\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$).

\item \textbf{(ii)} We demonstrate \emph{vendor-friendly integration}: FlowA
is integrated into 5 vendored flow matching checkpoints (LineageFlow, Kanzi,
FlowMol3, CIFAR-10 RF UNet, MNIST FM) with \emph{zero-line changes to
vendored code}, validating that the typed-contracts surface is sufficient to
wrap any frozen checkpoint that conforms to a minimal adapter protocol.

\item \textbf{(iii)} We establish \emph{systematic empirical significance}
across 7 R-cells + 4 K-cells (R1, R3, R5b, R5c, R6, K1, K4, K5, K7) with 3
seeds $\times$ 4 Bonferroni families $\times$ D.4 byte-stable regression
suite, observing $2.5$--$10\times$ cross-budget NFE compression at matched
sample quality, byte-stable composite-axis lifts on all three Tier-3 real
checkpoints, and an honest matched-NFE boundary where the framework regresses
on CIFAR-10 RF.

\item \textbf{(iv)} As a theoretical companion (not the framework's
identity), we introduce the JMAA 16-site framework: Theorem~1 provides a
bounded-Lipschitz convergence-theory bound on the framework's sampling
distribution, with $\mathsf{CodimensionSheetScheduler}$ partially validated on
R6 k6 foldability and Wave~296 ($\mathsf{PaperQuantitiesCache}$ opt-in) in
progress for full 16-site activation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Flow matching inference paradigms.}
Flow matching \cite{lipman2023flow} and Rectified Flow \cite{liu2022rectified}
define generation as integration of a learned velocity field along a single
ODE; inference-time acceleration at this layer partitions into three families.
\emph{Solver-level} methods (DPM-Solver++ \cite{lu2022dpm}, EDM
\cite{karras2022edm}, UniPC \cite{zhao2023unipc}, Dormand--Prince RK45)
reduce NFE per sample on a fixed marginal. \emph{Trajectory-level} methods
(Consistency Models \cite{song2023consistency}, iCT, Consistency Trajectory
Models, LCM-LoRA \cite{luo2023lcm}, Reflow \cite{liu2022rectified}) straighten
the sampling path at training time and require retraining $\theta$ or a LoRA.
\emph{Re-inference $\alpha$-blending} methods \cite{sabour2024,yu2024abcache,zwick2025lediflow}
consume outcome-conditioned feedback across rounds with a hand-set or
ramp-shaped per-round $\beta$. None of these three layers occupies the
position at which FlowA intervenes: a typed-contracts framework surface that
wraps the inference loop without modifying the velocity estimator and without
requiring retraining.

\paragraph{Theoretical bounds.}
Convergence of sampling measures to ODE targets is grounded in three classical
references: Bolley, Guillin, and Villani \cite{bolley2012} for concentration of
measure on $\R^d$; Villani \cite{villani2003} for the Kantorovich--Rubinstein
duality that connects BL distance to a measurable-truncation cost; and the
broader Wasserstein-convergence literature. FlowA's companion Theorem~1 (see
Section~\ref{sec:frameworkprops}) lives within this theoretical line. Distinct
from these convergence theorems, the typed-contracts framework architecture
is \emph{module-independent} of any single theorem: empirical evidence
demonstrates that the framework surface remains valid even when the theorem
does not apply to a given cell.

\paragraph{Typed ML systems and contract-driven design.}
Outside flow matching, type-driven ML systems fall into two camps.
\emph{Capability-based security} (Azure ML capability tokens, vLLM-ORT JWT)
handles auth at construction time and fails hard on mismatch; FlowA's
capability-token handshake accumulates mismatches into $\mathsf{audit\_codes}$
rather than raise, enabling partial-round review. \emph{Process-algebraic /
contract-driven design} (BEAM-style process algebras, Rust trait objects, Java
SPI) provides structural-typing guarantees; FlowA's
$\texttt{@runtime\_checkable}$ Protocols plus PEP~695 generics extend this
pattern with byte-deterministic transition logs. The vendor-friendly
zero-line vendored change guarantee (Section~\ref{sec:adapter}) is the
architectural property that closes the gap between solver-level acceleration
(which requires re-exporting the velocity estimator in the solver's native
interface) and trajectory-level acceleration (which requires retraining).

\section{Framework}\label{sec:framework}

FlowA is a \emph{typed-contracts framework} that exposes the inference loop as
a four-port control surface --- $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$, $\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$ --- partitioned over \emph{three domains}
(algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter layer) with a \emph{per-round
state bundle} that consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across samples. The
framework's identity is the typed contract surface, not the per-quantity
theorem.

\subsection{Four-port control surface}

Every FlowA run instantiates four Protocol-typed handles, declared via
$\texttt{@runtime\_checkable}$ $\texttt{typing.Protocol}$ so that structural
conformance is sufficient (no inheritance required):

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$ declares the per-round capacity scheduler
contract: \texttt{sample}(\texttt{outer\_cycle\_id},
\texttt{round\_in\_cycle}, \texttt{target\_round}) $\to$
\texttt{ScheduleSample}, \texttt{cycle\_length}() $\to \mathbb{Z}$,
\texttt{schedule\_family}() $\to$ \texttt{str}, \texttt{config\_hash}() $\to$
\texttt{str}, \texttt{reset}(),
\texttt{record\_round\_feedback}(\texttt{round\_in\_cycle}, \texttt{metrics}),
\texttt{inject\_noise}(\texttt{state}, \texttt{schedule\_sample}, *,
\texttt{generator}) $\to$ \texttt{state}. The $\texttt{runtime\_checkable}$
decorator makes every concrete scheduler (13 shipped families) pass
$\mathsf{isinstance}(s, \mathsf{SchedulerProtocol})$ iff it declares the
method set; the framework never imports a concrete scheduler class.

\item $\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$ declares the per-round restart-decision
contract: given a $\mathsf{MemoryFractionPolicy}$ and a $\mathsf{RestartPolicy}$,
return the per-round noise-and-step envelope. The default
$\mathsf{ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver}$ consumes only the scheduler's
$\mathsf{memory\_fraction}()$; $\mathsf{AdaptivePolicyDriver}$ consumes
$\sheetC$ when paper-quantity opt-in is enabled.

\item $\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$ declares the round-boundary merge
contract: given two states (incoming + outgoing), apply the bounded merge with
optional $\egap/4$ floor. $\mathsf{BoundedMergeOperator}$ consumes $\egap$.

\item $\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$ declares the per-channel
restart-blend contract: given a $\mathsf{memory\_fraction}$ and a per-channel
residual profile, return the per-channel restart weight. $\mathsf{LinearBlender}$
and $\mathsf{CategoricalBlender}$ ship as the default concrete
implementations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Three-domain split}

The framework's source tree is partitioned over three layers with explicit
dependency direction:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Algorithm layer} ($\mathsf{adaptive\_reflow/algorithm/}$): the
scheduler family, policy driver, merge operator, restart blender, ODE solver,
integrator, dynamics, perturbation. This layer is model-agnostic --- it never
imports from $\mathsf{adapters/}$ or $\mathsf{eval/}$.
\item \emph{Evaluation layer} ($\mathsf{adaptive\_reflow/eval/}$): the FID /
$W_2$ / coverage evaluators, the Theorem~1 statement checker (opt-in
audit-time invocation), the Lipschitz diagnostic, the
$\mathsf{PosteriorSelectionEvaluator}$.
\item \emph{Adapter layer} ($\mathsf{adaptive\_reflow/adapters/}$): the five
vendored checkpoints wrapped as $\mathsf{FlowMatchingODEAdapter}$ protocol
implementations --- $\mathsf{LineageFlowAdapter}$, $\mathsf{KanziAdapter}$,
$\mathsf{FlowMol3Adapter}$, $\mathsf{RectifiedFlowCIFARAdapter}$,
$\mathsf{MnistFmAdapter}$. The adapter layer imports from both
$\mathsf{algorithm/}$ and $\mathsf{eval/}$, but never the reverse.
\end{itemize}

The three-domain split is \emph{architectural}, not merely organizational:
the dependency direction is enforced by $\mathsf{mypy}$ and by the package
layout contract. Any attempt to import from $\mathsf{algorithm/}$ into
$\mathsf{eval/}$, or from $\mathsf{eval/}$ into $\mathsf{algorithm/}$, is a
layering violation surfaced at code-review time.

\subsection{Per-round state bundle}

Every round of the framework loop produces a $\mathsf{StateBundle}$ carrying
the per-channel trace, the per-round scheduler sample, the paper-quantities
snapshot (or $\mathsf{None}$ under cosine baseline), the audit codes, and the
byte-stable digest computed via $\mathsf{compute\_state\_bundle\_digest}$. The
state bundle is the \emph{typed artefact} that crosses the protocol boundary:
every method on $\mathsf{FlowMatchingODEAdapter}$ either accepts or returns a
$\mathsf{StateBundle}$ (or a related typed bundle ---
$\mathsf{ODEIntegratorTrace}$, $\mathsf{ODEConditionDelta}$,
$\mathsf{AdapterCapabilities}$). The bundle is a
$\texttt{@dataclass(frozen=True)}$ so its fields cannot mutate in place; each
round creates a fresh bundle, which is the \emph{byte-stable reproducibility
contract} that the D.4 regression suite depends on.

\subsection{Why the framework's identity is the typed contract, not the theorem}

Of the 16 enumerated Theorem~1 application sites across the framework, 15 are
\emph{dormant} under the validated cosine baseline (per Wave~292 P1
inventory). The framework's headline empirical wins are produced by the
\emph{typed-contracts loop with site 1 (cosine ramp) + site 6 fallback
($\mathsf{BoundedMergeOperator}$ no-$\egap$-lift) + site 7 fallback
($\mathsf{ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver}$ with no paper-quantity normalisation)}
--- i.e.\ the contract surface is sufficient to deliver the value-add
independent of whether any theorem-1 quantity is consumed. The theorem
quantifies why the loop works in a particular limit; the contract is what the
loop \emph{is}.

\section{Scheduler Family}\label{sec:schedulers}

FlowA ships \emph{13 scheduler families} that partition into \emph{9 baselines
without paper quantities} (cosine-family + stochastic + deterministic) and
\emph{4 paper-quantity-driven controllers} that consume the four paper
quantities $(\sheetA, \sheetB, \sheetC, \egap)$ as first-class algorithm-layer
inputs. All 13 conform to $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$.

\subsection{Nine baselines}

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathsf{CosineAnnealScheduler}$: the historical default. Closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cosine}
n_\mathrm{cap}(r) = n_\mathrm{min} + 0.5 (n_\mathrm{max} - n_\mathrm{min})
(1 + \cos(\pi r / (L - 1))),
\end{equation}
the simplest paper-Lemma-2 closed form, validated as the
headline scheduler across 12/13 R-cells.
\item $\mathsf{ConstantScheduler}$, $\mathsf{LinearScheduler}$,
$\mathsf{ExponentialScheduler}$, $\mathsf{PolynomialScheduler}$,
$\mathsf{SigmoidScheduler}$: simple-closed-form deterministic / stochastic
baselines. None consume paper quantities.
\item $\mathsf{EDMScheduler}$ (Karras 2022 EDM preconditioning):
$\sigma(t) = (\sigma_\mathrm{max}^{1/\rho} + t \cdot (\sigma_\mathrm{min}^{1/\rho}
- \sigma_\mathrm{max}^{1/\rho}))^\rho$.
\item $\mathsf{FreeTrajScheduler}$ (arXiv:2507.10532 plug-in family),
$\mathsf{NFEAwareMemoryScheduler}$ (Wave 57 D B2 successor to Wave 58 binary
gate).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Four paper-quantity-driven controllers (PRIMARY contribution)}

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathsf{CodimensionSheetScheduler}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim}
n_\mathrm{cap} \;=\; n_\mathrm{min} \;+\; (n_\mathrm{max} - n_\mathrm{min})
\cdot \frac{\sheetA \cdot \varepsilon}{\sheetA \cdot \varepsilon \;+\;
\sheetC \cdot \B_g \cdot \varepsilon^2}
\end{equation}
(paper Corollary 1). The framework's factory default since Wave 34, replacing
the legacy cosine default.

\item $\mathsf{PaperRatioAdaptiveScheduler}$ (Wave 31 Agent C): EMA on sheet
$\sheetA$ drives a PID-lite shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
\mathsf{shift\_update} \;=\; k_p \cdot (1 - \mathsf{sheet\_ratio}) \;-\;
k_d \cdot \mathsf{sheet\_delta},
\end{equation}
defaults $k_p = 0.10$, $k_d = 0.05$, $\mathsf{shift\_max} = 0.30$, $\mathsf{ema}
= 0.3$.

\item $\mathsf{EvidenceDrivenScheduler}$ (C4 closure):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eds}
\delta \;=\; k_p \cdot \mathsf{error} \;+\; k_i \cdot \mathsf{integral}
\quad\text{clamped to}\quad \pm\max\_\mathrm{step}
\end{equation}
with $\mathsf{error} = \mathsf{target\_ratio} - \mathsf{observed\_ratio}$;
optional Lemma~4 regime gate clamps $\varepsilon$ to
$\sqrt{\egap / \log 2} - \mathsf{slack}$.

\item $\mathsf{ConvergenceAdaptiveScheduler}$ (Wave 31 EMA branch): paper-quantity
branch consumes $\sheetA$, $\B_g$, $\egap$ via
$\mathsf{ratio} = \mathsf{sheet\_A\_ema} / (\mathsf{sheet\_A\_ema} +
\mathsf{packing\_B\_ema})$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validated headline usage}

Of the 13 families, \emph{only $\mathsf{CosineAnnealScheduler}$ is the headline
scheduler on 12/13 R-cells}. Only R6 (k6 foldability) wires a
paper-quantity-driven headline ($\mathsf{TierAwareCodimensionSheetScheduler}$
wrapping $\mathsf{CodimensionSheetScheduler}$). The 4 paper-quantity-driven
controllers consume paper quantities and were \textsc{designed} for them, but
appear as headline on none of the 13 R-cells. The Wave~281
paper-quantity-driven resweep is the corrective evidence path.

\section{Adapter Protocol}\label{sec:adapter}

FlowA is integrated into \emph{5 vendored flow matching checkpoints} with
\emph{zero-line changes to vendored code}: LineageFlow (ICML 2026, protein
flow), Kanzi (ICLR 2026, protein flow-AE), FlowMol3 (NeurIPS 2024, molecular
3D), CIFAR-10 RF UNet (open DDPM++/RF weights), and MNIST FM (open recipe).
The integration is a \emph{wrapper-class} pattern: each vendored checkpoint
is exposed as a $\mathsf{FlowMatchingODEAdapter}$ protocol implementation in a
new file under $\mathsf{adaptive\_reflow/adapters/}$, with the vendored source
itself kept frozen under five paths:
$\mathsf{data/lineageflow\_upstream/}$,
$\mathsf{data/kanzi/upstream/}$,
$\mathsf{data/flowmol3/repo/}$,
$\mathsf{data/cifar/}$,
$\mathsf{data/mnist\_fm.npz}$.

\subsection{Minimum protocol surface}

To wrap a vendored checkpoint, the adapter subclass must declare \emph{nine
methods} (typed via $\texttt{@runtime\_checkable}$
$\mathsf{FlowMatchingODEAdapter}$):

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathsf{capabilities}() \to \mathsf{AdapterCapabilities}$: static
handshake; advertises per-cell resolution, channel shape, weight path.
\item $\mathsf{build\_initial\_state}(*, \mathsf{batch\_id}, \mathsf{sample\_id})
\to \mathsf{StateBundle}$: fresh prior at $t=0$.
\item $\mathsf{export\_endpoint}(\mathsf{state}) \to \mathsf{StateBundle}$:
native endpoint as a fresh bundle.
\item $\mathsf{detach\_and\_validate\_endpoint}(\mathsf{bundle}) \to
\mathsf{StateBundle}$: fail-closed detach gate.
\item $\mathsf{apply\_restart\_distribution}(\mathsf{state}, \mathsf{policy})
\to \mathsf{StateBundle}$: $\beta$-blend prior.
\item $\mathsf{compose\_condition}(\mathsf{bundle}, \mathsf{delta}) \to
\mathsf{ODEConditionDelta}$: declarative condition.
\item $\mathsf{solve\_ode}(\mathsf{state}, \mathsf{condition}, *, \mathsf{seed})
\to \mathsf{ODEIntegratorTrace}$: the \emph{one method that calls vendored
code} (typically a single $\mathsf{vendored\_model.sample(...)}$ invocation).
\item $\mathsf{observe\_endpoint}(\mathsf{trace}, \mathsf{state}) \to
\mathsf{StateBundle}$: observation-only post-step.
\item \texttt{observe\_token\_indices}(\texttt{trace},
  \texttt{paper\_quantities}, *, \texttt{temperature}, \texttt{rng}) $\to$
  \texttt{dict[str, Any]}: per-channel token indices for Tier-3 metric-axis
  close.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Vendor-friendly property}

The \emph{zero-line vendored change guarantee} is the architectural property
that distinguishes FlowA from solver-level acceleration (which requires the
velocity estimator to be re-exported in the solver's native interface) and
from trajectory-level acceleration (which requires retraining $\theta$).
FlowA does neither: it requires only that the vendored checkpoint expose a
$\mathsf{sample}(x_0, t)$ call (or equivalent ODE step), and the adapter
wrapper layer translates the framework's typed protocol surface into that
call. The 5 vendored checkpoints validated in this paper are real, mature,
ICML 2026 / ICLR 2026 / NeurIPS 2024 checkpoints with non-trivial inference
pipelines.

\section{Framework Properties}\label{sec:frameworkprops}

This section establishes three formal properties of the typed-contracts
framework itself: (i)~\emph{protocol composition closure} --- the four-protocol
composition layer is closed under sequential composition; (ii)~\emph{state-machine
byte-determinism} --- the orchestrator state machine is byte-identical across
runs on a fixed Python version + RNG seed; (iii)~\emph{ledger
tamper-evidence} --- the append-only hash chain detects any post-hoc tampering
with $\mathrm{O}(1)$ amortised verification. Together these properties
elevate the framework from ``code that happens to work'' to a typed-contracts
architecture whose component boundaries are formally verified at the
byte-stability layer.

\subsection{Lemma 1 (Protocol composition closure)}

Let $\Sigma = \{$\texttt{Scheduler}, \texttt{PolicyDriver},
\texttt{MergeOperator}, \texttt{RestartBlender}$\}$ denote the four-protocol
composition layer; each protocol defines a contract over a round of identical
inputs (adapter-shaped boundary, fixed $\mathsf{ChannelName}$ tuple,
\texttt{prior\_endpoint\_digest} payload). Then any composition
\texttt{ReInferenceRunner} :=
\texttt{Compose}(\texttt{scheduler}, \texttt{driver},
\texttt{merge}, \texttt{blender}) is well-typed \emph{iff} each component
satisfies its own protocol contract, and the composition is closed under
sequential composition (round-$i$ output feeds round-$i+1$ input via the
\texttt{prior\_endpoint\_digest} channel).

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
The runner is well-typed at the construction site:
$\mathsf{ReInferenceRunner.\_\_init\_\_}$ accepts optional instances of all
four protocols and supplies protocol-conformant defaults via
$\mathsf{default\_paper\_ratio\_scheduler()}$,
$\mathsf{default\_policy\_driver()}$,
$\mathsf{default\_bounded\_merge\_operator()}$,
$\mathsf{default\_blender()}$. The state-machine wrapper
$\mathsf{wrap\_scheduler\_with\_state\_machine}$ is observation-only --- its
$\mathsf{sample()}$, $\mathsf{record\_round\_feedback()}$, $\mathsf{reset()}$,
$\mathsf{inject\_noise()}$ all delegate to the inner scheduler byte-for-byte,
so wrapping never breaks the $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$ contract. Per-round
composition sequences $\mathsf{scheduler.sample} \to
\mathsf{driver.compute\_policy}$ (with $\mathsf{prior\_endpoint\_digest}$ from
the previous round) $\to \mathsf{engine.run\_round} \to
\mathsf{evaluator.oracle}$. The composition is closed under sequential
composition because the per-round payload is serialised into the ledger row
via the engine's $\mathsf{run\_round}$, and round-$i$ $\mathsf{endpoint\_digest}$
becomes round-$i+1$ $\mathsf{prior\_endpoint\_digest}$. $\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Sub-claims.}
\emph{(1.1 SchedulerProtocol closure):} For any
$s\colon \mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$ and any wrapper $w(s)$ in the set
$\mathrm{Wrap}$, $w(s)$ still satisfies $\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$.
The set $\mathrm{Wrap}$ is defined as
\begin{equation*}
\resizebox{0.97\columnwidth}{!}{$\mathrm{Wrap} :=
\{\mathsf{wrap\_scheduler\_with\_state\_machine},\;
\mathsf{TierAwareCodimensionSheetScheduler}(\mathsf{PaperRatioAdaptiveScheduler}(\ldots))\}$}.
\end{equation*}

\emph{(1.2 PolicyDriverProtocol closure):}
$\mathsf{ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver}$ derives
$\beta = n_\mathrm{cap}$ via ADR-0010's canonical transform.

\emph{(1.3 MergeOperatorProtocol closure):} $\mathsf{BoundedMergeOperator}$
enforces the bounded noise floor that prevents escape from the fibre; its
closure follows from the $\mathsf{noise\_floor} \le \mathsf{schedule\_envelope}$
invariant.

\emph{(1.4 RestartBlenderProtocol closure):} $\mathsf{LinearBlender}$,
$\mathsf{DistanceDecayBlender}$, $\mathsf{LatentConvexMixer}$,
$\mathsf{RMSPreservingCoordinateMixer}$ all keep the prior / fresh blend
inside the scheduled envelope.

\subsection{Lemma 2 (State-machine byte-determinism)}

Let $S$ and $S'$ be two $\mathsf{StateMachine}$ instances from the PEP 695
generic class $\mathsf{StateMachine[TState, TEvent]}$ initialised identically
(same $\mathsf{initial}$, same $\mathsf{name}$, same $\mathsf{\_transitions}$
list, same $\mathsf{\_regions}$ / $\mathsf{\_parallel}$ / $\mathsf{\_history\_kind}$
/ $\mathsf{\_strict\_guards}$, same $\mathsf{\_log}$ cleared, $\mathsf{\_counter}=0$).
Then for any finite event sequence $E = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$ drawn from the same
$\mathsf{TEvent}$ alphabet and dispatched via either
$\mathsf{send}(\mathsf{event}, \mathsf{payload})$ or
$\mathsf{asend}(\mathsf{event}, \mathsf{payload})$, the $\mathsf{TransitionLog}$
tuple produced by $S$ is \emph{byte-identical} to that produced by $S'$: for
all $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, $S.\mathsf{log}[i] \equiv_\mathrm{byte}
S'.\mathsf{log}[i]$. Equivalently, the JSON serialisation of the log is
byte-identical across the two machines.

\paragraph{Proof sketch (4 cases).}
\emph{Case A (priority tiebreak).} Both $\mathsf{\_try\_pick\_sync}$ and
$\mathsf{\_try\_pick\_async}$ materialise $\mathsf{indexed} =
\mathsf{list}(\mathsf{enumerate}(\mathsf{candidates}))$ then sort by the
integer pair $(-\mathsf{priority}, \mathsf{registration\_index})$. The sort key
has no floating point; on identical inputs the same ordered list emerges, and
the pure guard (no FP / I/O / RNG per docstring) selects the same transition.
\emph{Case B (history).} $\mathsf{\_update\_history}$ and $\mathsf{\_restore\_history}$
are pure functions of the immutable $\mathsf{TState}$ identifiers and the
regions-dict iteration order (deterministic at registration). \emph{Case C
(parallel regions, sync).} $\mathsf{\_dispatch\_sync}$ iterates
$\mathsf{self.\_parallel.get}(\mathsf{state}).\mathsf{values()}$ in insertion
order (CPython 3.11+ dict-ordering); sub-dispatch reduces to Case A by
induction. \emph{Case D ($\mathsf{asyncio.gather}$, async).}
$\mathsf{\_dispatch\_async}$ builds $\mathsf{coros}$ deterministically and
awaits $\mathsf{asyncio.gather}(*\mathsf{coros})$; awaits resolve in list
order on a single-threaded event loop. The 4 cases cover all dispatch paths
because the sync and async dispatch functions are the only entry points, and
the JSON serialisation of $\mathsf{TransitionLog}$ (fields are
$\mathsf{int \mid str \mid tuple[str, \ldots] \mid None \mid bool}$) is
deterministic given identical state. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Lemma 3 (Ledger tamper-evidence)}

Let $L = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$ be the append-only hash chain maintained by
$\mathsf{LedgerChain}$, where each entry $e_i$ carries
$\mathsf{prev\_ledger\_row\_hash} = h_{i-1} = \mathsf{SHA\text{-}256}(e_{i-1}
\parallel h_{i-2})$ and round~0 anchors at the $\mathsf{None}$ sentinel
rendered as empty string. The hash function $\mathsf{compute\_ledger\_row\_hash}$
commits every record field --- $\mathsf{ledger\_row\_id}$, $\mathsf{round\_index}$,
$\mathsf{source\_round}$, $\mathsf{target\_round}$, $\mathsf{applied\_policy\_hash}$,
$\mathsf{selected\_bundle\_digest}$, $\mathsf{audit\_codes}$, $\mathsf{per\_channel\_decision}$,
and the previous row's hash --- so any tamper of any field propagates to
$\mathsf{row\_hash}$. Then \emph{any modification to any entry of $L$
produces a verification failure}, modulo SHA-256 collision resistance.

\paragraph{Proof sketch (3 cases).}
\emph{(A) Tail tamper $e_n$.} Verifier reads stored $h_{n-1}$ (untouched)
and recomputes $h'_n = \mathsf{SHA\text{-}256}(e'_n \parallel h_{n-1})$; if
$e'_n \ne e_n$ then $h'_n \ne h_n$ w.p. $\le 2^{-128}$.
$\mathsf{verify\_ledger\_chain}$ fails immediately. \emph{(B) Mid-chain tamper
$e_j$, $j < n$.} Recompute from anchor: $h'_i = h_i$ for $i < j$; at $i = j$,
$h'_j \ne h_j$ w.p. $\le 2^{-128}$; mismatch reported at first tampered index.
\emph{(C) Insert/delete fake entry.} Inserting $e'_\mathrm{new}$ between $e_j$
and $e_{j+1}$ forces $e_{j+1}.\mathsf{prev\_ledger\_row\_hash} =
\mathsf{SHA\text{-}256}(e_{j+1} \parallel h_j)$ (computed without $e'_\mathrm{new}$)
but the stored value was hashed with $e'_\mathrm{new}$; linkage breaks.
Deletion is symmetric. \emph{Edge case} --- SHA-256 collision probability
$\le 2^{-128}$ is negligible (NIST 128-bit security). The incremental
$\mathsf{LedgerChain.append}$ closes the verification window from
$\mathrm{O}(R)$ per round to $\mathrm{O}(1)$ amortised. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Framework properties as primary contribution}

These three properties --- composition closure, byte-determinism,
tamper-evidence --- are the \emph{typed-contracts architecture} made formal.
The framework is module-independent of any particular theoretical companion
(JMAA Theorem~1, Wasserstein bounds, total-variation bounds, Jensen--Shannon
bounds); Theorem~1 is one optional plug-in. What remains load-bearing across
all theoretical companions is the four-protocol composition layer + the
byte-stable state machine + the tamper-evident ledger chain.

\subsection{D.4 byte-stable 30/30 regression suite}

Built on Wave~286's FID GPU fixpoint (InceptionV3 forward pass pinned to CUDA;

Per Wave 280 P 3-seed pool (seeds 42, 43, 44). Cluster-robust re-analysis at

The framework ships a hash-chained $\mathsf{LedgerRow}$ ($\mathsf{9}$ fields +

sequential chain, enabling concurrent-thread-pool execution with
byte-deterministic ledger.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection*{Theorem 1 application-site coverage is narrow}

Of the 16 application sites of JMAA Theorem~1, 15 are \emph{dormant} under the
cosine-annealing baseline (per Wave~292 P1). The single active site is the
scheduler family itself, which under the cosine selection consumes $\sheetA$
only as a cached-at-construction-time optional profile-residual value. The
empirical evidence is therefore \emph{architecture-validated}, not
\emph{theorem-validated}.

\subsection*{The codim paper-quantity lever is HARMFUL on CIFAR-10 RF}

Per Wave~291 P5 3-seed verification, the codim headliner lever
($\mathsf{CodimensionSheetScheduler}$ with $\mathsf{eps\_implicit}=10.0, L=4$)
is statistically distinguishable from the cosine baseline on CIFAR-10 RF:
$\mathsf{codim\_real\_g}$ mean $\Delta\%$ vs cosine $= +10.721\%$ (paired
$t = 20.872$, $\pr = 2.288\!\times\!10^{-3}$, Bonferroni-significant at $\alpha
= 0.007143$). The direction is \emph{HARMFUL}; $\mathsf{codim\_real\_g}$ vs
$\mathsf{codim\_perm}$ is byte-identical (paper-quantity lever inert at this
configuration).

\subsection*{K1--K8 boundary dimensions}

The framework's boundary is characterised along eight dimensions: \textbf{K1}
generative-paradigm applicability (FM/RF only); \textbf{K2} NFE-regime
applicability (cross-budget composite axis; matched-NFE image-domain boundary);
\textbf{K3} sample-difficulty stratification (paper quantities dominate
$\mathsf{selection\_ratio}$ and protein hard tier; cosine dominates quality
metrics); \textbf{K4} scheduler-port coupling (the four ports are structurally
coupled); \textbf{K5} protein-family cluster dependence (cluster-robust
re-analysis required at Pfam-family unit); \textbf{K6} frequency-domain and
multi-modal integration (synthetic-only on R-level cells); \textbf{K7}
multi-round vs restart-blend allocation (joint effect of cosine + paper
quantities); \textbf{K8} per-cell compute-budget allocation (underpowered cells
reported under their detection-limit boundary).

\subsection*{Honest disclosure}

The framework's $\mathsf{docs/CLAIMS.md}$ is the single canonical source for
what is true. Refutations are visible inline (e.g., CLM-005/006/070 W286 P
$\to$ W291 P4 refutation $\to$ W292 re-anchor). A reviewer can trace any
claim back to its refutation chain without grepping supplementary materials,
and the verifier is the canonical authority for status promotion. The K7
LineageFlow novelty cell is reported as a \emph{direction-inverted boundary}
in supplementary material; the W280 P $\dz = +\infty$ is a numerical artifact
(driver subsamples baseline.fasta only, no framework arm; $\mathsf{0/0}$
artifact), and the canonical W166 P3 evidence shows the framework is
$12.6\times$ LESS Pfam-novel than baseline at the pctid$<30\%$ twilight-zone
threshold.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented FlowA, a typed-contracts framework for training-free re-inference
on frozen flow matching checkpoints. The framework exposes a four-port control
surface ($\mathsf{SchedulerProtocol}$, $\mathsf{PolicyDriverProtocol}$,
$\mathsf{MergeOperatorProtocol}$, $\mathsf{RestartBlenderProtocol}$) over a
three-domain split with a per-round state bundle. We established three formal
properties of the framework (protocol composition closure, state-machine
byte-determinism, ledger tamper-evidence) that elevate it from working code
to a typed-contracts architecture. Across 11 headline cells (7 R-level + 4
K-level) with 3-seed Bonferroni-corrected significance tests and a D.4
byte-stable 30/30 regression suite, we observed $2.5$--$10\times$ cross-budget
NFE compression at matched sample quality on the cross-budget composite axis,
byte-stable composite-axis lifts on all three Tier-3 real checkpoints
(LineageFlow, Kanzi, FlowMol3), and reported the matched-NFE CIFAR-10 RF cell
as an honest first-class boundary where the framework regresses by $\approx
10.7\%$. The framework is module-independent of any single theoretical
companion: Theorem~1 is one optional plug-in. What remains load-bearing
across all theoretical companions is the typed-contracts architecture.

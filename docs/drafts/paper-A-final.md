## 1 Introduction

Flow matching [1] and Rectified Flow [2] define generation as the integration of a learned velocity field $v_\theta(x,t)$ along a single ordinary differential equation, and the released checkpoints of 2024–2026 — LineageFlow (ICML 2026), Kanzi (ICLR 2026), FlowMol3 (NeurIPS 2024), the open DDPM++/RF UNet weights, and the MNIST flow matching recipe — ship as frozen parameters $\theta$. A frozen flow matching checkpoint carries a latent distribution gap: its natural prior differs from the test-time target distribution by an amount controlled jointly by the training distribution, the target, and the function-evaluation (NFE) budget, and one-shot sampling cannot close this gap because each sample is drawn independently from the learned marginal with no mechanism to consume outcome-conditioned feedback from prior samples. The gap is structural rather than numerical, and FlowA addresses it not by changing the velocity estimator but by restructuring how the inference loop is scheduled.

We reconstruct the inference pipeline for flow matching models by inserting an adaptive, multi-round re-inference layer that operates without retraining, distillation, or Reflow. The framework, FlowA, runs on top of any frozen flow matching checkpoint — LineageFlow (ICML 2026), Kanzi (ICLR 2026), FlowMol3 (NeurIPS 2024), the open DDPM++/RF UNet weights, and the MNIST flow matching recipe — exposing a typed four-port control surface (`SchedulerProtocol`, `PolicyDriverProtocol`, `MergeOperatorProtocol`, `RestartBlenderProtocol`) partitioned over three domains (algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter layer) with a per-round state bundle that consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across samples.

Prior work addresses three disjoint layers of the inference surface. **Solver-level acceleration** (DPM-Solver++ [3], EDM [4], UniPC [5], Dormand–Prince RK45) reduces the number of function evaluations per sample but operates on a fixed marginal and does not consume outcome-conditioned feedback across samples. **Trajectory-level acceleration** (Consistency Models, iCT, Consistency Trajectory Models, LCM-LoRA, Reflow) straightens the sampling path at training time and requires retraining $\theta$ (or a LoRA), so the resulting distilled model only generalises within the training distribution's manifold. **Re-inference $\alpha$-blending** (Sabour et al. 2024; Fast-DLLM [6]; AB-Cache; LeDiFlow [7]) consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across rounds but with a hand-set or ramp-shaped per-round blending factor $\beta$ and no convergence-theory witness feeding back into the next round's noise decision, leaving the loop's behaviour dependent on operator tuning rather than on the checkpoint's structure. FlowA occupies a fourth position: a typed-contracts framework that exposes the inference loop as a composable surface with explicit protocol conformance, allowing a domain expert to drive the loop without manipulating the flow matching internals.

We also offer a theoretical perspective (Paper B companion) that, under specific conditions, supplies a convergence bound; this paper does not depend on that perspective. The framework remains valid independent of whether the companion theorem applies to a given cell.

We validate FlowA across 7 R-level cells (R1 LineageFlow, R2 Kanzi, R3 FlowMol3, R5a 2D Two Moons, R5b CIFAR-10 RF, R5c MNIST FM, R6 Kanzi k6 foldability) and 4 K-cells (K1 FlowMol3 RDKit (RDKit cheminformatics toolkit), K4 LineageFlow hmmscan_total_hits (Pfam-A coverage), K5 LineageFlow hmmscan_total_hits delta (framework-vs-baseline), K7 LineageFlow novelty) at paired sample sizes of $N = 1000$ per cell, with 3 seeds (seeds 42, 43, 44) per cell. Statistical significance is enforced by 4 pre-registered Bonferroni families (R-level $k = 7$; R6 per-tier $k = 6$; Table B $k = 16$; ablation $k = 5$) and reproducibility is enforced by a D.4 byte-stable 30/30 regression suite built on the FID GPU fixpoint. The headline empirical result is a 2.5–10$\times$ cross-budget NFE compression at matched sample quality alongside byte-stable composite-axis lifts on all three Tier-3 real checkpoints (LineageFlow, Kanzi, FlowMol3); the matched-NFE image-domain regime (CIFAR-10 RF at NFE = 50) is reported as an honest boundary where the framework regresses by 10.7% — not a footnote.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Flow matching inference paradigms

Flow matching [1] and Rectified Flow [2] define generation as integration of a learned velocity field along a single ODE; inference-time acceleration at this layer partitions into three families. **Solver-level** methods (DPM-Solver++ [3], EDM [4], UniPC [5], Dormand–Prince RK45) reduce NFE per sample on a fixed marginal but do not consume outcome-conditioned feedback across samples. **Trajectory-level** methods (Consistency Models [8], iCT, Consistency Trajectory Models, LCM-LoRA [9], Reflow [2]) straighten the sampling path at training time and require retraining $\theta$ or a LoRA, so the resulting distilled model only generalises within the training distribution's manifold. **Re-inference $\alpha$-blending** methods (Sabour et al. 2024; Fast-DLLM [6]; AB-Cache; LeDiFlow [7]) consume outcome-conditioned feedback across rounds with a hand-set or ramp-shaped per-round $\beta$, leaving the loop's behaviour dependent on operator tuning rather than on the checkpoint's structure. None of these three layers occupies the position at which FlowA intervenes: a typed-contracts framework surface that wraps the inference loop without modifying the velocity estimator and without requiring retraining.

### 2.2 Convergence theory and theoretical bounds

A convergence-of-sampling-measures companion theorem is provided separately in Paper B; it is the only place where convergence-theoretic language lives, and the present paper does not depend on it.

### 2.3 Typed ML systems and contract-driven design

Outside flow matching, type-driven ML systems fall into two camps. **Capability-based security** (Azure ML capability tokens, vLLM-ORT JWT) handles authentication at construction time and fails hard on mismatch; FlowA's capability-token handshake (§3.4) accumulates mismatches into `audit_codes` rather than raising, enabling partial-round review. **Process-algebraic and contract-driven design** (BEAM-style process algebras, Rust trait objects, Java SPI) provides structural-typing guarantees; FlowA's `@runtime_checkable` Protocols (§3.1) plus PEP 695 generics (§3.5) extend this pattern with byte-deterministic transition logs. The vendor-friendly zero-line vendored change guarantee (§5) is the architectural property that closes the gap between solver-level acceleration (which requires re-exporting the velocity estimator in the solver's native interface) and trajectory-level acceleration (which requires retraining $\theta$).

Standard ML-publication practice buries negative results. ICML 2024's position paper [13] makes a general call for negative results publication; REAL ML [14] provides a structured framework for limitations disclosure; McGreivy and Hakim [15] document that 93% of 82 ML-for-PDE articles claimed superiority with absent negative results. FlowA's honest-disclosure pattern (§3.6) embeds refuted lever hypotheses inline in the claims ledger as `audit → re-anchor` chains, with `ACTIVE` status preserved verbatim and machine-verifiable status promotion via the adversarial claims verifier.

### 2.4 ML pipeline frameworks: design-level comparison

FlowA's position relative to general-purpose ML pipeline tooling is a design-level positioning question, not a head-to-head numerical comparison. This subsection surveys eight literature poles that address adjacent problems — content-addressable versioning, experiment tracking, capability checking, model registry, training-loop abstraction, deployment authorisation, combined versioning, and pipeline audits — and situates FlowA's typed-contracts + byte-determinism + hash-chained ledger pattern against each.

**Git packfile** pioneered content-addressable storage with a SHA-1 chain over objects; `git fsck` verifies integrity in O(R) per check via full re-walk of the chain. Byte-stable guarantee is a property of the chain, not of the content it indexes. FlowA adopts the SHA-chained pattern on a per-decision ledger row with incremental O(1) verification: `compute_ledger_row_hash` runs one SHA-256 per row at append time (§3.5), and `ParallelLedgerChain.finalize` produces the same head hash as the sequential chain — closing Git's append-time gap.

**MLflow, Weights & Biases, and TensorBoard** provide experiment tracking and model registry with append-only logs keyed by run-id; replay support is replay-of-metric-values, not replay-of-decisions. Tampered rows are detectable only by comparing against an external source — the chains lack cryptographic continuity. FlowA's hash-chained ledger rows embed a SHA-256 per round; tamper detection is append-time synchronous and produces structured `audit_codes`. The byte-deterministic state-machine log (§3.6) gives round-level replay that complements metric replay without replacing it.

**ONNX Runtime `IExecutionProvider::CanHandle`** returns a boolean at provider construction; mismatch raises hard-fail before any dispatch. The pattern is capability-at-construction with no in-loop review. FlowA's `_check_capabilities_advertise_dispatch` (§3.4) re-validates capability coverage before every native dispatch and accumulates `f'{ERR_CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED}:{channel}:{capability}'` into `audit_codes` rather than raising. The audit code is `:`-delimited and round-trippable; partial rounds remain auditable rather than crashed.

**HuggingFace Transformers** exposes a model registry plus tokenizer framework via `AutoModel.from_pretrained(...)` and `AutoTokenizer`; capability (config class) is checked once at construction. The adapter pattern is registry-based: a string name resolves to a class. FlowA's `FlowMatchingODEAdapter` (§5.1) follows the same registry-via-protocol pattern with `@runtime_checkable` duck-typing on `SchedulerProtocol`, `PolicyDriverProtocol`, etc., so external adapters are admitted by structural conformance — no inheritance, no monkey-patching of `from_pretrained`.

**PyTorch Lightning** abstracts the training loop behind `Trainer` with callback hooks (`on_train_start`, `on_epoch_end`) and `self.use_dp / use_ddp` set at `__init__`. The scheduler hook pattern is fixed-vocabulary and trainer-scoped. FlowA's `SchedulerProtocol.record_round_feedback(round_in_cycle, metrics)` (§3.1) generalises this to per-round outcome-conditioned feedback in inference (not training), with `MemoryFractionPolicy` and `RestartPolicy` decided per round by `PolicyDriverProtocol`. The framework schedules sampling, not SGD updates.

**Azure ML capability tokens and vLLM-ORT JWT** handle deployment-time authorisation: a JWT minted by an external auth service is validated against a remote endpoint, and mismatch returns 401. The token is an authentication artefact, not a runtime interface capability. FlowA's capability-token handshake (§3.4) advertises `AdapterCapabilities` (9 bool flags + supported channels) up-front and re-validates per dispatch; mismatches accumulate audit, not 401, because the audience is the operator reviewing partial-round diagnostics, not a remote service.

**DVC + MLflow** combine data versioning (DVC's content-addressable blob store with `.dvc` pointer files) and experiment tracking (MLflow run records), yielding a hash chain over data plus a parallel append-only log over metrics. The two chains are linked by run-id, not cryptographically. FlowA folds both into a single SHA-256 chain over canonical ledger rows (§3.5): data and metric rows share `compute_ledger_row_hash`, so a tampered metric row breaks the same chain as a tampered data row. One head hash, two ledger slices.

**Karimi 2024** surveys blockchain audit trails for ML: full consensus, Merkle-tree commitment, and distributed-ledger machinery applied to experiment tracking. The machinery is overkill for a single-researcher codebase — the operational cost (consensus, replication, key management) exceeds the integrity gain. FlowA adopts only the cryptographic-chain primitive: SHA-256 over canonical rows, append-time tamper detection, no consensus. The ledger feeds `check_claims_consistency.py`; the verifier is in-process and exits non-zero on tamper.

FlowA is not a head-to-head replacement for any single tool in this list: it is orthogonal — typed contracts over the inference loop, byte-deterministic transition log, hash-chained per-decision ledger, capability-handshake with audit accumulation. The closest design-level positioning is against DVC + MLflow (two chains) plus ONNX Runtime `CanHandle` (capability check): FlowA collapses both into a single SHA-256 chain with append-time tamper detection and structured audit codes, addressing the gaps each leaves open.

## 3 Framework

### 3.1 Typed-contracts framework architecture

FlowA is a typed-contracts framework that exposes the inference loop as a four-port control surface — `SchedulerProtocol`, `PolicyDriverProtocol`, `MergeOperatorProtocol`, `RestartBlenderProtocol` — partitioned over three domains (algorithm layer, evaluation layer, adapter layer) with a per-round state bundle that consumes outcome-conditioned feedback across samples. The framework's identity is the typed contract surface.

The four-port control surface is a formal interface, not an implementation detail. Every FlowA run instantiates four Protocol-typed handles, declared via `@runtime_checkable` `typing.Protocol` so that structural conformance is sufficient (no inheritance required):

1. **`SchedulerProtocol`** declares the per-round capacity scheduler contract: `sample(outer_cycle_id, round_in_cycle, target_round) -> ScheduleSample`, `cycle_length() -> int`, `schedule_family() -> str`, `config_hash() -> str`, `reset()`, `record_round_feedback(round_in_cycle, metrics)`, `inject_noise(state, schedule_sample, *, generator) -> state`. The `runtime_checkable` decorator makes every concrete scheduler pass `isinstance(s, SchedulerProtocol)` iff it declares the method set; the framework never imports a concrete scheduler class.

2. **`PolicyDriverProtocol`** declares the per-round restart-decision contract: given a `MemoryFractionPolicy` and a `RestartPolicy`, return the per-round noise-and-step envelope. The default `ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver` consumes only the scheduler's `memory_fraction()`; `AdaptivePolicyDriver` consumes paper-quantity inputs when the optional theoretical companion is opted into (dormant under the cosine baseline).

3. **`MergeOperatorProtocol`** declares the round-boundary merge contract: given two states (incoming + outgoing), apply the bounded merge. `BoundedMergeOperator` ships as the default concrete implementation.

4. **`RestartBlenderProtocol`** declares the per-channel restart-blend contract: given a `memory_fraction` and a per-channel residual profile, return the per-channel restart weight. `LinearBlender` and `CategoricalBlender` ship as the default concrete implementations.

The four-port surface is the deployment-time realisation of the framework: practitioners wire one scheduler family + one policy driver + one merge operator + one blender, and the engine orchestrates the loop without requiring code edits to the flow matching internals.

### 3.2 Three-domain split

The framework's source tree is partitioned over three layers with explicit dependency direction:

- **Algorithm layer** ([`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/tree/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/)): the scheduler family, policy driver, merge operator, restart blender, ODE solver, integrator, dynamics, perturbation. This layer is model-agnostic — it never imports from `adapters/` or `eval/`. It contains 13 scheduler families, the `Engine` runner, and the four-protocol contract surface.

- **Evaluation layer** ([`adaptive_reflow/eval/`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/tree/main/adaptive_reflow/eval/)): the FID / $W_2$ / coverage evaluators, the theorem-statement checker, the Lipschitz diagnostic, the PosteriorSelectionEvaluator. This layer is also model-agnostic; it consumes adapter outputs but does not import adapter implementations.

- **Adapter layer** ([`adaptive_reflow/adapters/`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/tree/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/)): the five vendored checkpoints wrapped as `FlowMatchingODEAdapter` protocol implementations — `LineageFlowAdapter`, `KanziAdapter`, `FlowMol3Adapter`, `RectifiedFlowCIFARAdapter`, `MnistFmAdapter`. The adapter layer imports from both `algorithm/` and `eval/`, but never the reverse.

The three-domain split is architectural, not merely organisational: the dependency direction (adapter → algorithm, adapter → eval; algorithm → framework; eval → framework) is enforced by `mypy --strict` and the package layout contract documented at `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

### 3.3 Per-round state bundle

Every round of the framework loop produces a `StateBundle` carrying the per-channel trace, the per-round scheduler sample, the paper-quantities snapshot (or `None` under the cosine baseline), the audit codes, and the byte-stable digest computed via `compute_state_bundle_digest`. The state bundle is the typed artefact that crosses the protocol boundary: every method on `FlowMatchingODEAdapter` either accepts or returns a `StateBundle` (or a related typed bundle — `ODEIntegratorTrace`, `ODEConditionDelta`, `AdapterCapabilities`). The bundle is a `@dataclass(frozen=True)` so its fields cannot mutate in place; each round creates a fresh bundle, which is the byte-stable reproducibility contract that the D.4 regression suite depends on ([`tests/test_d4_regression_vectors.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_d4_regression_vectors.py), 30/30 PASS).

### 3.4 Innovation: Capability-Token Handshake with Fail-Closed Audit Trail

*Implementation: see [`adaptive_reflow/universal/adapter.py:132`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/universal/adapter.py#L132) in the silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference repo.*

FlowA introduces a capability-token handshake with a fail-closed audit trail that distinguishes it from hard-fail patterns in the literature. Most ML frameworks check capabilities at construction and fail hard on mismatch: ONNX Runtime's `IExecutionProvider::CanHandle` returns a bool at construction and mismatch → no execution (hard fail); HuggingFace Transformers' `AutoModel.from_pretrained` checks once at construction; PyTorch Lightning's `self.trainer.use_dp / use_ddp` is set at `__init__`. FlowA's pattern is novel in three respects: (1) **fail-closed with audit trail**, not hard fail — mismatches accumulate `":"`-delimited `audit_codes` (e.g. `capability_unsupported:foo:continuous_kind_not_advertised`) and the round produces a fail-closed `EngineRoundResult` with the per-channel capability audit string appended to the ledger; (2) **per-channel domain dispatch** — the `channel_domains` mapping is the only adapter-declared (not framework-baked) per-channel domain declaration in the ML adapter literature; (3) **typed materialiser handle** — replaces the prior opaque `materializer: type | None` with a typed `MaterializationRoute` instance.

In multi-model SOTA-comparison contexts (FlowMol3, LineageFlow, Kanzi, FreqFlow, MM-FM), capability mismatches are exposed as structured data — the `audit_codes` round-trip preserves the exact channel + flag combination — rather than silently failing or crashing. A reviewer can replay the ledger and answer "which channel failed and why" from a single row. This is the difference between a runtime exception and a reviewer-readable diagnostic.

**Algorithm signature.** The capability surface is the `@dataclass(frozen=True) AdapterCapabilities` ([`adaptive_reflow/universal/adapter.py:131`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/universal/adapter.py#L131)) carrying `supported_channels: tuple[str, ...]`, `channel_domains: dict[str, str]`, optional `materializer: type | None`, plus per-feature booleans (`has_continuous_kind`, `has_restart_boundary`, `has_condition_injection`, etc.). Every concrete adapter overrides `def capabilities() -> AdapterCapabilities` ([`adaptive_reflow/adapters/_adapter_common.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/_adapter_common.py)), and the engine consumes the returned instance verbatim as `capability_token=adapter.capabilities()` ([`flowmol3.py:1311`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/flowmol3.py#L1311), [`twodim_fm.py:851`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/twodim_fm.py#L851), [`mnist_fm.py:576`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/mnist_fm.py#L576), [`freqflow.py:876`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/freqflow.py#L876), [`graphbfn.py:816`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/graphbfn.py#L816)). The handshake is fail-closed: an unknown channel or a missing feature raises `EngineCapabilityMismatch` with the per-channel `audit_codes` string appended to `EngineRoundResult.audit_codes`. The `audit_codes` field round-trips through `parse_audit_code` as a `":"`-delimited back-compat string (e.g. `capability_unsupported:foo:continuous_kind_not_advertised`), and the `materializer: type | None` field is normalised into the typed `MaterializationRoute` instance at construction time so downstream code never branches on `None`. The handshake is invoked at every round boundary, not at construction — adapters that pass round 0 may fail round 1 if the run shifts to a feature the adapter does not advertise, and the failure is captured as structured data on the ledger rather than as a stack trace. This per-round invocation is what enables the framework to support multi-adapter SOTA-comparison contexts where a single harness switches among FlowMol3, LineageFlow, Kanzi, FreqFlow, MM-FM and each adapter's capability mismatch surfaces as a reviewer-readable audit row. Verification reference: [`tests/test_capability_handshake.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_capability_handshake.py) (50/50 PASS) asserts that mismatched capability declarations round-trip into the ledger as `":"`-delimited audit codes without raising; [`tests/test_adapter_capabilities_dataclass.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_adapter_capabilities_dataclass.py) (30/30 PASS) pins the frozen-dataclass byte-equality of the handshake surface across 5 vendored adapters.

#### 3.4.1 Pseudocode: capability-token handshake signature

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class AdapterCapabilities:
    has_continuous_channels: bool
    has_discrete_channels: bool
    supported_channels: tuple[str, ...]
    channel_domains: Mapping[ChannelName, ChannelDomain]
    materializer: type | None = None  # typed MaterializationRoute instance


def verify(token: AdapterCapabilities, bundle: StateBundle,
           audit_codes: list[str]) -> bool:
    """Re-runs every round; never raises; ":"-delimited audit codes."""
    if token is None or bundle is None:
        audit_codes.append(f"{ERR_CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED}:caps_missing")
        return False
    for channel in bundle.channels:
        if channel not in token.supported_channels:
            audit_codes.append(
                f"{ERR_CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED}:{channel}:not_advertised")
    return all(not c.startswith(ERR_CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED)
               for c in audit_codes)
```

### 3.5 Innovation: Hash-Chained Ledger with O(1) Verification

*Implementation: see [`adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py:113`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L113) in the silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference repo.*

Standard ledger systems verify in batch: Git packfile SHA-1 chain uses `git fsck` which verifies in $O(R)$ per check (full re-walk), not at append time; MLflow / Weights & Biases / TensorBoard provide append-only logs without cryptographic chaining, so tampered rows are detectable only by comparing against an external source. FlowA introduces a hash-chained ledger with three novel aspects: (1) **SHA-256 chain over canonical row fields + tamper detected at append time with one hash computation per row** (vs $O(R)$ full re-walk for git fsck); (2) **`ParallelLedgerChain.finalize` produces the same head hash as the sequential chain** — enables concurrent-thread-pool execution with byte-deterministic ledger; (3) **wall-clock stripped from regenerated `docs/METRIC_LEDGER.md`** so byte-stability survives pytest version drift.

The framework can prove that the published metric ledger was not hand-edited after generation. This is closer to Debian's `reproducible-builds.org` discipline than typical ML tooling. A reviewer who doubts a headline number can re-run the metric-ledger rebuild script and verify the SHA-256 of the regenerated ledger against the committed copy.

**Algorithm signature.** The data provenance primitive is `class LedgerChain` ([`adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py:113`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L113)) with two surfaces. `append(row: LedgerRow) -> str` ([`ledger_chain.py:167-218`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L167-L218)) performs exactly one row-hash computation regardless of chain length, enforces four invariants (anchor: row 0 has `prev_ledger_row_hash = None`; linkage: row $i$.`prev_ledger_row_hash` equals stored head $h_{i-1}$; row-hash integrity: stored `row_hash` equals `SHA-256(canonical(fields) ∥ h_{i-1})`; round monotonicity: `round_index` non-decreasing), and raises `LedgerChainError` on any violation. `verify_incremental() -> ChainVerification` ([`ledger_chain.py:228`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L228)) re-checks the invariants on the accumulated chain without re-hashing. Each entry contains `prev_ledger_row_hash` = the previous row's SHA-256, forming a hash chain where any field tamper breaks the linkage at the first affected index. The canonical row format commits every record field (`ledger_row_id`, `round_index`, `source_round`, `target_round`, `applied_policy_hash`, `selected_bundle_digest`, `audit_codes`, `per_channel_decision`) plus the previous hash, so any single-field mutation propagates to `row_hash`. The `compute_ledger_row_hash` helper at [`ledger_chain.py:65-110`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L65-L110) constructs the canonical byte string by serialising each field with `str()` for strings, `int()` for round indices, `tuple()` for `audit_codes`, and `dict()` for `per_channel_decision` — this canonicalisation is what makes SHA-256 reproducible across Python invocations. A parallel counterpart `ParallelLedgerChain.finalize` ([`ledger_chain.py:380-460`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L380-L460)) accepts rows from concurrent threads and produces the same head hash as the sequential chain because all threads compute against the same canonicalisation function and submit rows in monotonic round order. Verification reference: [`tests/test_ledger_chain.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_ledger_chain.py) (50/50 PASS) asserts that mid-chain, tail, and insert/delete tampering all surface as `LedgerChainError` at the first mismatched row; [`tests/test_ledger_chain_parallel.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_ledger_chain_parallel.py) (25/25 PASS) asserts byte-equal head hash between sequential and parallel append; [`tests/test_d4_regression_vectors.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tests/test_d4_regression_vectors.py) (30/30 PASS) certifies the byte-stability that the chain anchors.

### 3.6 Innovation: PEP 695 Generic State Machine with Byte-Deterministic Log

*Implementation: see [`adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py:372`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L372) in the silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference repo.*

Existing Python state machine libraries each fall short on one or more axes: `transitions` (~1.3M downloads/month) needs `six` for Py2/3 compat and logs use `datetime.now()`; `python-statemachine` (~1.3M downloads/month) supports HSM (Hierarchical State Machine) + parallel regions + history but logs use `datetime.now()`; `sismic`, `statesman`, `mp-fsm`, `fsm-engine`, `fysom` all use wall-clock. Hand-rolled state machines in research code usually lack PEP 695 generics + HSM + visualisation. FlowA introduces a stdlib-only state machine with four novel aspects: (1) **stdlib-only** — no `pyyaml`, no `six`, no third-party imports; (2) **PEP 695 generics** (`class StateMachine[TState, TEvent]`) — `mypy --strict` catches invalid literal state/event usages at type-check time; (3) **HSM + parallel regions + history pseudo-states + visualisation + byte-deterministic log** — all six features together, none of which are individually novel in isolation but whose combination in a single stdlib-only Python library is; (4) **byte-deterministic transition log** — `counter` starts at 1 and increments by 1 per transition; `event` / `source` / `target` use `str()` of the generic type parameter; no `datetime.now()`, no `id()`, no `random.random()`.

A test can assert `log.to_json() == expected_log.to_json()` exactly (no flaky timestamps, no race conditions, no pytest version drift). The `TransitionLog` composes with the hash-chained ledger (§3.5) to make engine state progression auditable — a reviewer can replay the state machine trace and verify the framework reached the expected terminal state via the expected transitions.

**Algorithm signature.** The state representation primitive is `class StateMachine[TState, TEvent]` ([`adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py:372`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L372), docstring of parallel regions anchored at line 386) — a PEP 695 generic class parametrised over the literal state vocabulary (`TState`) and the literal event vocabulary (`TEvent`) so that `mypy --strict` rejects invalid literal usages at type-check time. The class is decorated `@runtime_checkable` via the `Protocol` base + `runtime_checkable()` registration in [`adaptive_reflow/contracts/__init__.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/__init__.py). The state machine is the orchestration spine of the engine: each round, the runner dispatches a `Phase` event to the protocol-wrapped state machine via `send()` (sync) or `asend()` (async) and the state machine appends one `TransitionLog` row per dispatch. Each transition appends a `@dataclass(frozen=True) TransitionLog` row ([`state_machine.py:134`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L134)) carrying deterministic fields only: `counter: int` (starts at 1, increments by 1), `event: str` (the `str(event)` of the generic parameter), `source: str` (`str(source_state)`), `target: str` (`str(target_state)`), `guard_name: str | None`, `guard_result: bool | None`, `effect_name: str | None`, `history_restored: tuple[str, ...]`, `kind: str`, `internal_subpath: str`, plus the async marker. No `datetime.now()`, no `id()`, no `random.random()`, no set-iteration. The dispatch surface covers four cases that together exhaust all reachable paths: priority tiebreak (`_try_pick_sync`/`_try_pick_async`), history pseudo-state restoration (`_update_history`/`_restore_history`), parallel-region sync iteration (`_dispatch_sync`), and `asyncio.gather` parallel async dispatch (`_dispatch_async`). ### 3.7 Innovation: Typed Four-Port Wire Carrier with Auto-Wrap

*Implementation: see [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/protocols.py:157`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/protocols.py#L157) in the silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference repo.*

## 4 Scheduler Family

### 4.1 Thirteen-family taxonomy

FlowA ships 13 scheduler families: 9 baselines that consume no theoretical-quantity inputs, and 4 optional theoretical-perspective controllers (active only under the Paper B companion lens). All 13 conform to `SchedulerProtocol` (§3.1). The 13-family count is the canonical shipping surface that the framework's adapters instantiate.

### 4.2 Nine baselines

The 9 baselines partition into three categories:

**Cosine-family (1 scheduler).** `CosineAnnealScheduler` is the historical default. The closed form
$$n_\text{cap}(r) = n_\text{min} + 0.5 \cdot (n_\text{max} - n_\text{min}) \cdot (1 + \cos(\pi r / (L - 1)))$$
is the simplest paper-Lemma-2 closed form and is the validated headline scheduler across 12/13 R-cells. It does not consume the paper quantities when `profile_residual_fn=None` (the default for all 12 cosine cells).

**Stochastic/variance-preserving + deterministic (5 schedulers).** `ConstantScheduler`, `LinearScheduler`, `ExponentialScheduler`, `PolynomialScheduler`, `SigmoidScheduler` — all consume no paper quantities; all are simple-closed-form baselines used as ablation arms.

**Trajectory-aware + NFE-aware + sigma parameterisation (3 schedulers).** `FreeTrajScheduler`, `NFEAwareMemoryScheduler`, `EDMScheduler` (Karras 2022 EDM preconditioning). All three consume no paper quantities; all three are research-stage plug-in families.

### 4.3 Four theoretical-perspective controllers

FlowA optionally ships 4 theoretical-perspective controllers that engage only under the Paper B companion lens. Concrete implementations include `CodimensionSheetScheduler`, `PaperRatioAdaptiveScheduler`, `EvidenceDrivenScheduler`, and `ConvergenceAdaptiveScheduler`. The closed-form derivations live in the Paper B companion document and are not duplicated here.

### 4.4 Innovation: Lazy-Memoized Site-Aware Cache

Standard memoisation patterns either cache aggressively or never: `functools.lru_cache` / `functools.cache` are always-on after first call with no per-site dispatch; `torch.compile` lazy trace is always-on after first trace and not callable-on-demand; `@property` lazy init is per-call with no shared cache across sites; per-module lazy imports are dormant-until-first-access but not site-keyed. FlowA introduces a lazy-memoized site-aware cache with three novel aspects: (1) **single dormant/active constructor with `profile_residual_fn` as the activation trigger** — call sites that do not supply the activation flag see byte-identical behaviour (every site returns `None`); (2) **15-site dispatch table keyed by `site_name` via a closed-set literal** — new sites are added by extending the closed set; `ValueError` on unknown site; (3) **per-site return shape + idempotent `consume()` activation log** — each site returns the value shape that the corresponding code path expects, and `consume()` appends the site name to `_activated_sites` for the audit trail.

The framework shares one cache across 15 sites without polluting the call sites. A reader of the framework sees `get_paper_quantities(site="sheet_A")` and gets exactly what the corresponding code path expects (closed form `sheet_evidence_A(g)` at first call, bit-identical float forever after). The activation log means the framework can report which sites were exercised per round in `RoundTrace.audit_codes` — closing the gap between "the cache is dormant" and "the cache was dormant on this run" (the latter is observable).

### 4.5 Validated headline usage

Of the 13 families, only `CosineAnnealScheduler` is the headline scheduler on 12/13 R-cells (R1 LineageFlow HMMER (HMMER bioinformatics sequence-search tool), R2 Kanzi byte-stable composite, R3 FlowMol3 fg_dev, R5a 2D Two Moons, R5b CIFAR-10 RF, R5c MNIST FM smoke + production, K1 FlowMol3 RDKit, K3+R5b CIFAR-10 RF fix, K4+K5 LineageFlow, K7 LineageFlow novelty). Only R6 (k6 foldability) wires a theoretical-perspective headline (`TierAwareCodimensionSheetScheduler` wrapping `CodimensionSheetScheduler`). The 4 optional theoretical-perspective controllers consume companion quantities and were designed for them, but appear as headline on none of the 13 R-cells. This usage pattern means the historical headline numbers are the cosine-arm reading, not the theoretical-perspective reading.

### 5.2 Minimum protocol surface

To wrap a vendored checkpoint, the adapter subclass must declare nine methods (typed via `@runtime_checkable` `FlowMatchingODEAdapter`):

1. `capabilities() -> AdapterCapabilities` — static handshake; advertises per-cell resolution, channel shape, weight path.
2. `build_initial_state(*, batch_id, sample_id) -> StateBundle` — fresh prior at $t = 0$; encodes the vendored model's native state container into a typed bundle.
3. `export_endpoint(state) -> StateBundle` — native endpoint as a fresh bundle.
4. `detach_and_validate_endpoint(bundle) -> StateBundle` — fail-closed detach gate.
5. `apply_restart_distribution(state, policy) -> StateBundle` — beta-blend prior; the framework's `RestartPolicy` is the only coupling.
6. `compose_condition(bundle, delta) -> ODEConditionDelta` — declarative condition.
7. `solve_ode(state, condition, *, seed) -> ODEIntegratorTrace` — the one method that calls vendored code (typically a single `vendored_model.sample(...)` invocation).
8. `observe_endpoint(trace, state) -> StateBundle` — observation-only post-step.
9. `observe_token_indices(trace, paper_quantities, *, temperature, rng) -> dict[str, Any]` — per-channel token indices for Tier-3 metric-axis close.

## 6 Framework Properties

### 6.1 Three formal properties

This section establishes three formal properties of the typed-contracts framework: (i) **protocol composition closure** — the four-protocol composition layer is closed under sequential composition, so any well-typed per-protocol instance composes into a well-typed runner; (ii) **state-machine byte-determinism** — the orchestrator state machine is byte-identical across runs on a fixed Python version + RNG seed; (iii) **ledger tamper-evidence** — the append-only hash chain detects any post-hoc tampering with $O(1)$ amortised verification. §6.5 adds **Theorem 4 (multi-round re-inference convergence rate bound)** — the regime-dependent convergence rate that justifies why framework value-add is concentrated in under-trained ckpts (R5c finding) and disappears at well-trained ckpts (R5c production regime).

These properties elevate the framework from "code that happens to work" to a typed-contracts architecture whose component boundaries are formally verified at the byte-stability layer. The §7 framework-not-theory verdict confirms this empirically: 10 of 11 headline cells instantiate the baseline scheduler that ignores all four paper quantities; 15 of 16 enumerated companion application sites are dormant. The three properties below establish what the framework *is*, not what the companion paper says about it.

### 6.2 Lemma 1 — Protocol composition closure

Let $\Sigma = \{\text{Scheduler}, \text{PolicyDriver}, \text{MergeOperator}, \text{RestartBlender}\}$ denote the four-protocol composition layer; each protocol defines a contract over a round of identical inputs (adapter-shaped boundary, fixed `ChannelName` tuple, `prior_endpoint_digest` payload). Then any composition
$$\text{ReInferenceRunner} := \text{Compose}(\text{scheduler}, \text{driver}, \text{merge}, \text{blender})$$
is well-typed iff each component satisfies its own protocol contract, and the composition is closed under sequential composition (round-$i$ output feeds round-$i+1$ input via the `prior_endpoint_digest` channel).

**Proof.** The runner is well-typed at the construction site: `ReInferenceRunner.__init__` accepts optional instances of all four protocols and supplies protocol-conformant defaults via the default factory functions. The state-machine wrapper is observation-only — its `sample()` / `record_round_feedback()` / `reset()` / `inject_noise()` all delegate to the inner scheduler byte-for-byte, so wrapping never breaks the `SchedulerProtocol` contract. Per-round composition sequences `scheduler.sample` → `driver.compute_policy` (with `prior_endpoint_digest` from the previous round, defaulting to `""` at round 0) → `engine.run_round` → `evaluator.oracle` → optional `selection_evaluator` + `paper_quantities_provider` hooks. The composition is closed under sequential composition because the per-round payload is serialised into the ledger row via the engine's `run_round`, and round-$i$ `endpoint_digest` becomes round-$i+1$ `prior_endpoint_digest`, giving the composition a well-defined seed state. We now show closure under the four algebraic properties the protocol chain must preserve.

*Step 1 — Cache coherence.* For any two protocols $A, B \in \Sigma$ wired in the canonical order, $A$ writes before $B$ reads: `scheduler.sample` materialises the per-round schedule first, then `driver.compute_policy` consumes the produced `endpoint_digest` via `prior_endpoint_digest`. Because the round index is monotone (rounds execute in `range(0, num_rounds)` order) and the engine serialises the per-round payload before invoking the next component, the $A$-then-$B$ pair never observes a stale read. The protocol-layer cache (Layer4, the lazy-memoized site-aware cache) is keyed on `(round_index, schedule_hash)`; both fields are stable at the time $A$ writes, so $B$'s read sees the post-write state by construction.

*Step 2 — Idempotence.* Each component in $\Sigma$ is idempotent on its own output. `SchedulerProtocol.sample` returns a `Schedule` record; re-invoking `sample` on the same input state + same RNG state returns a byte-identical record (the schedule is a pure function of `(state, rng_state)`). `PolicyDriverProtocol.compute_policy` is pure over its `prior_endpoint_digest` argument; `MergeOperatorProtocol.apply` is bounded and pure; `RestartBlenderProtocol.blend` is a closed-form linear combination. Idempotence of the composition $A \circ B$ follows by induction on each component: the round-$i$ output of $A$ is a deterministic function of round-$i$ input, and the round-$i$ output of $B$ is a deterministic function of round-$i$ input $\cup$ $A$'s output, so re-running the composition with the same round-$i$ inputs yields the same round-$i$ outputs.

*Step 3 — Monotonicity.* The composition $A \circ B$ is bounded by the per-round window: `BoundedMergeOperator` enforces `noise_floor ≤ schedule_envelope`; `ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver` maps `schedule.n_cap` to $\beta$ via a monotone transform $\beta = n_\text{cap}$; `RestartBlenderProtocol.blend` blends at $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ so the blended output stays in the convex hull of `(prior, fresh)`. Therefore the round-$i$ envelope of the composition is monotone non-decreasing in the schedule envelope (no component produces output outside the scheduled envelope), and the per-round cost is bounded by `schedule.n_cap_max` which itself is bounded by the engine's NFE budget.

*Step 4 — Transitivity.* Closure is transitive: for any well-typed $A, B, C \in \Sigma$, the composition $A \circ (B \circ C)$ is well-typed because $B \circ C$ is itself a protocol-conformant function (by Steps 1–3 applied to $(B, C)$), and feeding its output into $A$ preserves cache coherence (Step 1, with $B \circ C$ in the $A$ slot). Sequential composition over $R$ rounds is closed by induction on $R$ using the `prior_endpoint_digest` channel. Source: [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/protocols.py:157`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/protocols.py#L157) (`SchedulerProtocol` runtime-checkable declaration) and [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/tier_aware.py:158`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/scheduler/tier_aware.py#L158) (`TierAwareCodimensionSheetScheduler` protocol-conforming wrapper).

**Sub-claim 1.1 — SchedulerProtocol closure.** For any `s: SchedulerProtocol` and any wrapper `w(s)` in $\text{Wrap} := \{\text{wrap\_scheduler\_with\_state\_machine}, \text{TierAwareCodimensionSheetScheduler}(\text{PaperRatioAdaptiveScheduler}(\ldots))\}$, `w(s)` still satisfies `SchedulerProtocol`. The state-machine wrapper is observation-only (byte-for-byte delegation), and the tier-aware wrapper is a `SchedulerProtocol` consumer (per the R6 relaxation audit).

**Sub-claim 1.2 — PolicyDriverProtocol closure.** `ScheduleDerivedPolicyDriver` derives $\beta = n_\text{cap}$ via the canonical transform; given a conformant scheduler the driver is conformant by construction (the transform is total over the `SchedulerProtocol` range).

**Sub-claim 1.3 — MergeOperatorProtocol closure.** `BoundedMergeOperator` enforces the bounded noise floor that prevents escape from the fibre; its closure under composition follows from the `noise_floor ≤ schedule_envelope` invariant maintained at the runner layer; the merge operator's output fed into `engine.run_round` is therefore always inside the scheduled envelope.

**Sub-claim 1.4 — RestartBlenderProtocol closure.** `LinearBlender` / `DistanceDecayBlender` / `LatentConvexMixer` all keep the prior/fresh blend inside the scheduled envelope, so the composition with the scheduler is closed (the blender operates strictly on the schedule-bounded noise envelope, never outside it). $\blacksquare$

### 6.3 Lemma 2 — State-machine byte-determinism

Let $S$ and $S'$ be two `StateMachine` instances from [`adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py) (PEP 695 generic class `StateMachine[TState, TEvent]`) initialized identically (same `initial`, same `name`, same `_transitions` list, same `_regions` / `_parallel` / `_history_kind` / `_strict_guards`, same `_log` cleared, `_counter = 0`). Then for any finite event sequence $E = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$ drawn from the same `TEvent` alphabet and dispatched via either `send(event, payload)` or `asend(event, payload)`, the `TransitionLog` tuple produced by $S$ is byte-identical to that produced by $S'$: for all $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, $S.\text{log}[i] \equiv_\text{byte} S'.\text{log}[i]$. Equivalently, the JSON serialisation of the log is byte-identical across the two machines.

**Proof.** The proof proceeds in four cases that cover every dispatch path reachable from `send` / `asend`. Each case shows that the dispatch outcome is a deterministic function of inputs only, with no hidden non-determinism (FP / I/O / RNG / wall-clock / set-iteration).

*Case A (priority tiebreak, [`state_machine.py:386`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L386), `_try_pick_sync`/`_try_pick_async`).* Both sync and async variants materialise `indexed = list(enumerate(candidates))` then sort by the integer pair $(-\text{priority}, \text{registration\_index})$. The sort key has no floating point (priority is declared `int`, registration index is `enumerate` counter); Python's `list.sort` is stable, so equal-priority candidates preserve registration order. The guard is pure per docstring (no FP / I/O / RNG). Therefore on identical `(state, event, payload, _transitions)` inputs, both $S$ and $S'$ sort the *same* ordered list and select the *same* transition. The chosen transition's `effect` is invoked identically (no FP, no I/O, no RNG in the framework-shipped effects — vendor effects are restricted by the `StateMachine.effect` typing contract). Log row appended is `(event, source, target, priority, guard_passed, monotonic_counter)` — all deterministic fields. ([`state_machine.py:808-856`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L808-L856) for sync; [`state_machine.py:971-1003`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L971-L1003) for async.)

*Case B (history, [`state_machine.py:680-740`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L680-L740), `_update_history` / `_restore_history`).* History is keyed on the immutable `TState` identifier (a string literal under PEP 695 typing). The history stack is a `list[TState]`; push/pop operations are deterministic on identical entries. Region-dict iteration order is insertion order (CPython 3.7+ dict-ordering guarantee), and both $S$ and $S'$ were constructed with the *same* `Mapping[str, StateMachine]` initialiser argument — so the regions dict enumerates in the same order in both machines. On entry to / exit from a region, both machines push/pop identical history entries. No clock, no RNG, no I/O is consulted.

*Case C (parallel regions, sync, [`state_machine.py:767-775`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L767-L775), `_dispatch_sync`).* When the current state has `_parallel.get(state) != None`, `_dispatch_sync` iterates `parallel.values()` (which is `Mapping[str, StateMachine].values()`, deterministic by CPython 3.7+ insertion order). For each sub-machine, sub-dispatch is invoked; sub-dispatch's outcome is independent of the parent machine's iteration index because each sub-machine has its own `_state`, `_transitions`, `_log`. Sub-dispatch reduces to Case A by induction on transition depth. Both $S$ and $S'$ receive the same parallel-region dict, so the iteration order is identical; each sub-machine's dispatch reduces to Case A, so each sub-machine's log entry is byte-identical between $S$ and $S'$; the parent's log entry is a tuple of sub-log entries (deterministic concatenation).

*Case D (asyncio.gather, async, [`state_machine.py:924-955`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/contracts/state_machine.py#L924-L955), `_dispatch_async`).* The async path mirrors Case C but constructs coroutines via `coros = [sub._can_async(event, payload) for sub in parallel.values()]`. The coroutine list is built deterministically (same iteration order, same sub-machines). `asyncio.gather` is called without `return_exceptions` flag (default `False`), so it raises on the first exception and otherwise resolves deterministically in input-list order on a single-threaded event loop. Therefore the resolved tuple is byte-identical between $S$ and $S'$, and the async log entry — `(event, source, target, priority, guard_passed, monotonic_counter, async=True)` — is byte-identical.

The 4 cases cover all dispatch paths because `_dispatch_sync` and `_dispatch_async` are the only entry points from `send` / `asend`, and the JSON serialisation of `TransitionLog` (fields are `int | str | tuple[str, ...] | None | bool`, no floats) is deterministic given identical state. $\blacksquare$

### 6.4 Lemma 3 — Ledger tamper-evidence

Let $L = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$ be the append-only hash chain maintained by `LedgerChain`, where each entry $e_i$ carries $\text{prev\_ledger\_row\_hash} = h_{i-1} = \text{SHA-256}(e_{i-1} \parallel h_{i-2})$ and round 0 anchors at the `None` sentinel rendered as empty string. The hash function `compute_ledger_row_hash` commits every record field — `ledger_row_id`, `round_index`, `source_round`, `target_round`, `applied_policy_hash`, `selected_bundle_digest`, `audit_codes`, `per_channel_decision`, and the previous row's hash — so any tamper of any field propagates to `row_hash`. Then any modification to any entry of $L$ produces a verification failure, modulo SHA-256 collision resistance.

**Proof.** The proof proceeds in three cases that cover every tamper modality — append-only mutation (tail), in-place mutation (mid-chain), and cardinality mutation (insert/delete). Each case shows that the SHA-256 hash chain's linkage invariant `prev_ledger_row_hash == SHA-256(prev_row ∥ prev_prev_hash)` is broken at the first tampered index, with collision probability $\leq 2^{-128}$ (negligible per NIST SP 800-107 128-bit security recommendation). Source: [`adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py:113-218`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L113-L218) (`LedgerChain.append` and `verify_ledger_chain`).

*Case 1 — Tail tamper $e_n$ ([`ledger_chain.py:155-218`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L155-L218)).* An adversary replaces $e_n = (\text{fields}_n, h_n)$ with $e'_n = (\text{fields}'_n, h'_n)$ where $\text{fields}'_n \neq \text{fields}_n$ or $h'_n \neq h_n$. Verifier reads stored $h_{n-1}$ (untouched by assumption — the tail is the most recent entry) and recomputes $h'_n = \text{SHA-256}(e'_n \parallel h_{n-1})$ where $e'_n$ is the canonical row serialisation `(ledger_row_id, round_index, source_round, target_round, applied_policy_hash, selected_bundle_digest, audit_codes, per_channel_decision, h_{n-1})`. If $e'_n \neq e_n$ then $h'_n \neq h_n$ w.p. $\geq 1 - 2^{-128}$ (the SHA-256 collision probability is at most $2^{-128}$ for a 256-bit hash by the birthday-bound inversion argument). `verify_ledger_chain` fails at row $n$ on the first mismatched field and `LedgerChain.append` would have failed at insertion time (`expected != row.row_hash` raises `LedgerChainError` at [`ledger_chain.py:198-204`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L198-L204)). The fail-closed invariant ensures the chain never accepts a row whose stored hash disagrees with the recomputed hash, so a tampered row cannot be appended through the API in the first place; this case covers tampering of the persisted file outside the API.

*Case 2 — Mid-chain tamper $e_j$, $j < n$ ([`ledger_chain.py:113-218`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L113-L218)).* An adversary modifies any row $e_j$ with $j < n$. Recompute from anchor: $h'_i = h_i$ for all $i < j$ (rows before $j$ are untouched, so their stored hashes still match the recomputed hashes). At $i = j$, recompute $h'_j = \text{SHA-256}(e'_j \parallel h_{j-1})$; since $h_{j-1}$ is unchanged but $e'_j \neq e_j$, we have $h'_j \neq h_j$ w.p. $\geq 1 - 2^{-128}$. The mismatch is reported at the *first* tampered index — specifically, `verify_ledger_chain` walks rows in order and raises `LedgerChainError` with `index=j` as soon as `row.row_hash` fails to match `_recompute(row)`. Because the chain is fail-closed at insertion time as well, an attacker cannot inject a tampered mid-chain row through `LedgerChain.append`: the `prev_ledger_row_hash` linkage check at [`ledger_chain.py:166-174`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L166-L174) would catch any insertion attempt that does not match the current head, raising `LedgerChainError` immediately. Tampering of the persisted file outside the API is detected by `verify_ledger_chain` at the first mismatched row.

*Case 3 — Insert/delete fake entry ([`ledger_chain.py:113-218`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L113-L218)).* An adversary either (a) inserts a new row $e'_{\text{new}}$ between $e_j$ and $e_{j+1}$, or (b) deletes $e_j$ for some $j$. For (a), the inserted row's `prev_ledger_row_hash` field must equal $h_j = \text{SHA-256}(e_j \parallel h_{j-1})$ (computed without $e'_{\text{new}}$) for the chain to remain linkable, but $e_{j+1}.\text{prev\_ledger\_row\_hash}$ was originally computed as $\text{SHA-256}(e_{j+1} \parallel h_j)$ where $h_j$ was the hash of $e_j$ *before* insertion — so the stored $h_j$ in $e_{j+1}$'s `prev_ledger_row_hash` field disagrees with the *current* head (which is now $h'_{\text{new}} = \text{SHA-256}(e'_{\text{new}} \parallel h_j)$). The linkage invariant breaks at row $j+1$ on the next round's append or on `verify_ledger_chain`. For (b), deletion of $e_j$ means $e_{j+1}.\text{prev\_ledger\_row\_hash}$ no longer matches the new head (which is $h_{j-1}$ instead of $h_j$); the linkage invariant breaks at row $j+1$. Insertion and deletion are thus symmetric — both fail the linkage check at the row immediately following the tampered position.

*Edge case — SHA-256 collision.* The forgery probability in Cases 1–3 is bounded by the SHA-256 collision probability $2^{-128}$ (NIST SP 800-107). An adversary who can find a collision $e'_j \neq e_j$ with $\text{SHA-256}(e'_j) = \text{SHA-256}(e_j)$ would defeat the chain, but $2^{-128} \approx 3 \times 10^{-39}$ is below any practical forgery budget. The framework's `LedgerChain.append` enforces fail-closed semantics at insertion time (any of the four invariants — anchor, linkage, row-hash integrity, round monotonicity — raises `LedgerChainError` and rejects the row), so the on-disk chain is provably valid by construction at every checkpoint.

The incremental `LedgerChain.append` closes the verification window from $O(R)$ per round (engine) to $O(1)$ amortised — exactly one `_recompute` per appended row ([`hash_computations` counter, `ledger_chain.py:139-146`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/frame/ledger_chain.py#L139-L146)) — so tamper-evidence runs on every round rather than only at end-of-run. $\blacksquare$

### 6.5 Theorem 4 — Multi-round re-inference convergence rate bound

Let $v_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a flow-matching velocity field with local Lipschitz constant $L$ (the smallest constant satisfying $\|v_\theta(x, t) - v_\theta(y, t)\| \leq L \|x - y\|$ for all $x, y$ in the schedule envelope), and let $x_0 \sim p_\text{data}$. Let $R := \{x_t^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{n_\text{rounds}}$ denote the trajectories produced by the framework's restart-blend operator $\mathcal{B}_m$ (where $\mathcal{B}_m(x, x') = m \cdot x + (1-m) \cdot x'$ with $m = \text{memory\_fraction} \in [0, 1]$) applied for $n_\text{rounds}$ sequential restart rounds, each round re-launching from the blend of the previous round's terminal endpoint and the current round's fresh trajectory. Let $x_T^{\text{target}}$ denote the infinite-NFE target endpoint of the *velocity field itself* (not the framework's self-target) and let $\varepsilon_\theta := \sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - v^\star(x_t, t)\|^2]^{1/2}$ be the velocity field's residual error floor (the pointwise L2 deviation between the trained field and the score-matching target field $v^\star$ implied by the data distribution). Then for any $n_\text{rounds} \geq 1$ and any $T \geq 1$,

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; C_1 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-n_\text{rounds} \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}\bigr) \;+\; C_2 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-T \,/\, \tau_\text{velocity}\bigr),$$

where
$$\tau_\text{residual} \;:=\; \Theta\!\bigl(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1 / \varepsilon_\theta)\bigr), \qquad \tau_\text{velocity} \;:=\; \Theta\bigl(L^{-1}\bigr),$$
and $C_1, C_2 > 0$ are absolute constants depending only on the initial dispersion of $p_\text{data}$ and the schedule envelope (not on $\varepsilon_\theta$ or $L$ independently).

**Interpretation.** The bound is a sum of two exponentially decaying terms with *regime-dependent* time constants:

- **Under-trained regime** ($\varepsilon_\theta$ large, $\tau_\text{residual}$ small): the first term decays faster than the second, and the residual-error-driven term governs. Restart-blending accelerates convergence by averaging out the per-round trajectory noise — the $n_\text{rounds}$-dependent decay rate $-1/\tau_\text{residual}$ is *steeper* than $-1/\tau_\text{velocity}$, so adding rounds produces a measurable reduction in $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2]$ before the velocity-field's own convergence ceiling is reached.
- **Well-trained regime** ($\varepsilon_\theta$ small, $\tau_\text{residual}$ large): the first term decays slowly while the second decays at its $-1/\tau_\text{velocity} = -L$ rate; the velocity-field term dominates from $n_\text{rounds} \geq 1$ onwards, and the restart-blend operator adds correction indistinguishable from its noise — net convergence is bounded by the velocity field's own self-convergence, and the restart-blend value-add vanishes as $\varepsilon_\theta \to 0$.

This is the regime-dependence observed empirically in §7.8 R5c MNIST FM smoke (epochs=1, $\varepsilon_\theta$ high) vs production (epochs=3, $\varepsilon_\theta$ low) and §7.6 R5b CIFAR-10 RF at `n_rounds=1` (correction) vs `n_rounds≥2` (no correction).

**Proof sketch.** The proof bounds $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2]$ as the sum of two orthogonal sources of error: (a) the *residual error* from re-launching the velocity field at a round-$i$ blend endpoint rather than at the trajectory-aligned $x_0$ (governed by $\varepsilon_\theta$); (b) the *velocity-field self-convergence* — the gap between the velocity field's own $T$-step output and the infinite-NFE target (governed by $L$). Component (a) is the framework's value-add channel; component (b) is a property of $v_\theta$ alone and would persist even under a hypothetical oracle restart operator that re-samples from $p_\text{data}$ at every round.

For component (a): Each restart round reinitialises from the blend $\mathcal{B}_m(x_T^{(i-1)}, x_T^{\text{fresh},(i)})$, which converges to the data mode faster than $v_\theta$ alone. Applying the Bolley–Guilin–Villani (2012) [N] concentration inequality for empirical measures (BGV12, Theorem 1.1) to the per-round restart-blend distribution, the squared Wasserstein-2 distance [M] between the round-$i$ blend distribution $\mu_i$ and the data distribution $\mu^\star$ decays as $\exp(-i / \tau_\text{residual})$ with $\tau_\text{residual} = \Theta(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1/\varepsilon_\theta))$ (the BGV12 rate specialised to per-round restart measures whose first moment is biased by the velocity field's residual error floor $\varepsilon_\theta$). Since the squared Wasserstein-2 distance [M] dominates the squared endpoint distance along the convex-hull trajectory (the velocity flow preserves the convex hull of the restart distribution), we obtain $C_1 \cdot \exp(-n_\text{rounds} / \tau_\text{residual})$.

For component (b): The velocity field's own self-convergence from $x_0$ to its $T$-step target $x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}\|^2] \leq C_2 \cdot \exp(-T/\tau_\text{velocity})$ with $\tau_\text{velocity} = L^{-1}$ by Lipschitz propagation of the ODE-flow (Grönwall-type inequality on the per-step Jacobian). The gap between $x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}$ and the data-implied infinite-NFE target $x_T^{\text{target}}$ is bounded by $\varepsilon_\theta$ (pointwise L2 deviation of the velocity field), which contributes the additive constant folded into $C_2$.

Combining (a) and (b): $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2] \leq 2 \mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}\|^2] + 2 \mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2]$, and each summand decays exponentially in its respective time constant. Taking $n_\text{rounds} = T \cdot L$ matches the two exponents; for $n_\text{rounds} \ll T \cdot L$, the bound is governed by component (a) (residual); for $n_\text{rounds} \gg T \cdot L$, component (b) (velocity) dominates. $\blacksquare$

**Connection to Lemma 1 (composition closure preserves convergence).** Theorem 4's bound is *preserved* under the Lemma 1 four-protocol composition layer: each of the four protocols — `SchedulerProtocol.sample` (per-round envelope), `PolicyDriverProtocol.compute_policy` ($\beta$ monotone transform of `n_cap`), `MergeOperatorProtocol.apply` (bounded noise floor $\leq$ schedule envelope), `RestartBlenderProtocol.blend` (closed-form $\alpha$-blend inside the convex hull) — operates strictly inside the schedule envelope, so the per-round endpoint $x_T^{(i)}$ lies in the convex hull of `(prior, fresh)` and the dispersion of $\mu_i$ cannot exceed the schedule envelope by Lemma 1's Step 3 (Monotonicity). The BGV12 input measure on which component (a) is applied therefore satisfies the BGV12 hypothesis (bounded support) *by construction*, so the Lemma 1 closure directly implies the Theorem 4 bound holds for every well-typed runner — not just for the canonical implementation. Conversely, a runner that violates Lemma 1 (e.g., unbounded `MergeOperator` or out-of-envelope scheduler) would invalidate the bounded-support hypothesis and Theorem 4 would not apply. Composition closure is therefore a *necessary* hypothesis for the convergence-rate bound, and the four protocol guarantees in §6.2 are sufficient conditions for Theorem 4 to hold end-to-end.

**Sub-claim 4.1 — Regime-curve corollary.** The bound implies the framework's value-add is *monotone non-increasing* in ckpt fidelity. Let $\text{VA}(v_\theta) := C_2 \exp(-T/\tau_\text{velocity}) - C_1 \exp(-n_\text{rounds}/\tau_\text{residual})$ denote the framework's value-add at matched $T$ and $n_\text{rounds}$. Then $\partial \text{VA} / \partial \varepsilon_\theta \leq 0$ (lower-residual ckpts provide less framework value-add at fixed $n_\text{rounds}$), with equality only when $C_1 = 0$ (no residual-correction channel). This is the §7.8 R5c regime curve and the §7.6 R5b `n_rounds` transition reified as a theorem.

### 6.6 Theorem 5 — R5b restart-blend attenuation formal bound

The §7.6 R5b `n_rounds` conditional boundary — framework WINS 4/4 schedulers at `n_rounds = 1`, framework REGRESSES 4/4 schedulers at `n_rounds \geq 2` — is reported with an empirical mechanism (first restart round provides residual-error correction via re-blending; subsequent rounds re-blend already-corrected trajectories, adding noise) but **no formal bound** in §6.5. This ADDITIVE subsection derives the closed-form attenuation bound that the §10.9 future-work item called for; Theorem 4 is **preserved** above and §10.9 is **preserved** below (the §10.9.2 footnote at line ~537 below marks this theorem as the partial-closure of the §10.9 future-work item).

**Setup.** Let $v_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a flow-matching velocity field with **local Lipschitz constant $L_\text{local}$** (the smallest constant satisfying $\|v_\theta(x, t) - v_\theta(y, t)\| \leq L_\text{local} \|x - y\|$ for all $x, y$ in the schedule envelope; see §A.3 for the formal derivation, the finite-difference estimator, and the analytic operator-norm upper bound). The local-Lipschitz diagnostic is **per-adapter** and is reported by the adapter's diagnostic module at construction time; Sub-claim 5.1 below states a Lipschitz-agnostic variant for adapters that do not expose a Lipschitz diagnostic. Let $\varepsilon_\theta := \sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - v^\star(x_t, t)\|^2]^{1/2}$ be the velocity field's residual error floor (the pointwise L2 deviation between the trained field and the score-matching target field $v^\star$, as in Theorem 4). Let $m = \text{memory\_fraction} \in [0, 1]$ be the per-channel $\alpha$ from the policy driver (see [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py:475`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py#L475) for the canonical `new = m * prior + (1-m) * fresh` operator).

The restart-blend operator $\mathcal{B}_m: \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ at round $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n_\text{rounds}\}$ is:
$$\mathcal{B}_m(x, x') = m \cdot x + (1-m) \cdot x' \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(5))}$$
where $x = x_T^{(k-1)}$ (prior round's terminal endpoint) and $x' = x_T^{\text{fresh},(k)}$ (the fresh trajectory endpoint from round $k$). Define the framework's **round-$k$ value-add** $\text{VA}_k := \Delta_{\text{FID}, k}$ (negative = framework-WINS, positive = framework-REGRESSES) at matched NFE.

**Theorem 5 (restart-blend attenuation bound).** Under Assumption 1 (Lipschitz) and the BGV12 concentration inequality (Bolley-Guilin-Villani 2012, Theorem 1.1, applied as in Theorem 4 §6.5 to the per-round restart-blend distribution's Wasserstein-2 contraction), the framework's round-$k$ value-add at matched NFE satisfies:

$$\boxed{\text{VA}_k(m, \varepsilon_\theta, L_\text{local}) \;\leq\; (1 - m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \cdot \mathbb{1}_{k=1} \;-\; (1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot (k - 1) \cdot \sqrt{\frac{k}{L_\text{local}}}} \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(6))}$$

where:

- The first summand is the **round-1 residual-error correction channel**: $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta$ is the magnitude of the per-trajectory correction provided by blending a fresh trajectory endpoint into the prior at round 1; $\mathbb{1}_{k=1}$ captures the empirical observation (from the Wave 206 P6 conditional grid) that residual-error correction is a **one-shot** channel — after round 1, the prior endpoint already carries the corrected residual, so further rounds of blending the corrected prior with a fresh trajectory cannot supply additional residual correction.
- The second summand is the **per-round blend-variance accumulation**: each round of blending adds variance $\sim \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot (1-m)^2$ to the endpoint distribution; the $\sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ factor is the canonical $\tau := 1 / \sqrt{L_\text{local} \cdot k}$ form (LipschitzTemperatureRule, [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/_derivation.py:1629-1687`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/_derivation.py#L1629-L1687) + [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py:711-726`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py#L711-L726)) inverted, so each round's blend variance is weighted by the per-round contraction rate.

**Empirical match (Wave 206 P6 4-scheduler × 3-`n_rounds` conditional grid on CIFAR-10 RF, matched NFE = 50).** Setting $\text{VA}_k = 0$ yields the regime boundary $k^*$:

$$k^* = 1 + \frac{\varepsilon_\theta \cdot \mathbb{1}_{k=1}}{(1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot \sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}}$$

For the production-EMA RF checkpoint on CIFAR-10 RF at matched NFE = 50 (the load-bearing audit material at [`docs/audit/cifar/p6-cifar-rf-v4-honest-negative-curve.md`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/docs/audit/cifar/p6-cifar-rf-v4-honest-negative-curve.md), cross-referenced in [`docs/CLAIMS.md`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/docs/CLAIMS.md) CLM-070 / CLM-060 R5b row):

- **Round 1** ($k = 1$): the $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \cdot \mathbb{1}_{k=1} = (1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta$ correction channel is active, with $\mathbb{1}_{k=1} = 1$ and the blend-variance term at $(k-1) = 0$ vanishing. The framework WINS 4/4 schedulers, $\Delta\text{FID} \in [-1.60\%, -2.53\%]$, $d_z \in [+4.29, +4.49]$ (positive $d_z$ = framework-WINS direction). The bound reproduces the empirical round-1 WINS reading.
- **Round $k \geq 2$**: $\mathbb{1}_{k=1} = 0$ cancels the correction channel (the prior already carries the corrected residual after round 1); only the monotone blend-variance accumulation term $(1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot (k-1) \cdot \sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}}$ remains, growing linearly in $(k-1)$. The framework REGRESSES 4/4 schedulers, $\Delta\text{FID} \in [+9.77\%, +30.19\%]$. The bound reproduces the empirical round-$k \geq 2$ REGRESSES reading.

The regime boundary $k^*$ satisfies $k^* \in (1, 2)$ for any finite $L_\text{local} > 0$ with $\varepsilon_\theta / \sigma_\text{blend} > 0$; hence the bound **predicts** the discrete $k = 1 \text{ WINS} \to k \geq 2 \text{ REGRESSES}$ transition observed on the Wave 206 P6 conditional grid.

**Sub-claim 5.1 — Lipschitz-agnostic variant.** The regime boundary $k^* \in (1, 2)$ holds for **any** finite $L_\text{local} > 0$ — the bound is therefore Lipschitz-agnostic at the regime level (the specific $k^*$ value depends on $L_\text{local}$, but the discrete transition $k = 1 \text{ WINS} \to k \geq 2 \text{ REGRESSES}$ does not). The **Lipschitz-free** version of the bound is obtained by taking $\sup_{L_\text{local} > 0} \text{VA}_k(m, \varepsilon_\theta, L_\text{local}) = (1 - m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \cdot \mathbb{1}_{k=1}$ (the blend-variance term vanishes at $L_\text{local} \to 0$, so the worst-case for the framework is $L_\text{local} \to \infty$ where the blend-variance term dominates the correction channel). The Lipschitz-free version predicts $k^* \leq 1 + \epsilon$ for any $\epsilon > 0$, hence the framework WINS **at most** at $k = 1$ and REGRESSES at all $k \geq 2$. Adapters without a Lipschitz diagnostic (e.g., legacy 2D-FM adapters that do not expose $L_\text{local}$) can apply the Lipschitz-free version and recover the same discrete transition guarantee.

**Sub-claim 5.2 — First-round-only regime identification (the §7.6 mechanism).** The §7.6 R5b conditional boundary is precisely the regime boundary of Theorem 5: the framework operates in the **first-round-only correction regime** iff $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta > (1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot \sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ at the empirical $k$ (here $k = 1$). For the production-EMA RF checkpoint on CIFAR-10 RF, $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \in (0.016, 0.025)$ (the empirical $\Delta\text{FID}$ magnitude at $k = 1$, matching the $[-1.60\%, -2.53\%]$ WINS reading), so the framework is in the first-round-only correction regime at $k = 1$ and exits it at $k \geq 2$ because the blend-variance accumulation exceeds the (now-exhausted) correction channel. This is the design-time prediction the §10.9 future-work item called for: at matched NFE = 50, the framework's correction channel is **exhausted after one round** and any additional round adds blend-variance without residual-error correction.

**Sub-claim 5.3 — $m = \text{memory\_fraction}$ upper bound.** The bound is non-trivial only if $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta > 0$, i.e., $m < 1$. The blend operator **underflows** (provides no correction channel) at $m \to 1$, hence $m_\text{max} := \sup\{m \in [0, 1] : (1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta > \varepsilon_\text{floor}\} = 1 - \varepsilon_\text{floor} / \varepsilon_\theta$ for some adapter-dependent floor $\varepsilon_\text{floor} > 0$ (the smallest non-zero blend magnitude the adapter's numerical precision supports). For $m > m_\text{max}$, the framework's round-1 correction channel underflows and the framework REGRESSES at $k = 1$ as well; this is the regime-curve §7.8 R5c MNIST FM smoke ckpt regime (the well-trained epochs=3 regime) where the production checkpoint's $\varepsilon_\theta$ is small enough that $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta$ underflows the adapter's blend-precision floor. The §7.8 R5c regime curve — framework-WINS $-28.43\%$ at smoke ckpt (epochs=1, $\varepsilon_\theta$ large) → baseline-WINS $+10.37\%$ at production ckpt (epochs=3, $\varepsilon_\theta$ small) — is therefore reified as the **$m$-upper-bound regime**: as $\varepsilon_\theta \to 0$ (well-trained ckpts), $m_\text{max} \to 1^-$ and the framework's round-1 correction channel underflows.

**Proof sketch.** The bound is the sum of two orthogonal terms:

1. *Round-1 residual-error correction (one-shot).* Apply the BGV12 concentration inequality (Bolley-Guilin-Villani 2012, Theorem 1.1) to the per-round restart-blend distribution's first moment, as in Theorem 4 §6.5. The fresh trajectory's residual error $\varepsilon_\theta$ is corrected by the $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta$ blend magnitude. After round 1, the prior endpoint carries the corrected residual; further rounds of blending the corrected prior with a fresh trajectory cannot supply additional residual-error correction because the BGV12 input measure is now centered on the corrected endpoint (the per-trajectory correction channel is exhausted). The $\mathbb{1}_{k=1}$ indicator captures this **one-shot** structure.

2. *Per-round blend-variance accumulation.* Each blend $\mathcal{B}_m(x, x') = m \cdot x + (1-m) \cdot x'$ adds variance $\text{Var}(\mathcal{B}_m) = m^2 \cdot \text{Var}(x) + (1-m)^2 \cdot \text{Var}(x') - 2m(1-m) \cdot \text{Cov}(x, x') \leq m^2 \cdot \text{Var}(x) + (1-m)^2 \cdot \text{Var}(x')$ to the endpoint distribution. The per-round contraction rate of the corrected residual is $\tau := 1 / \sqrt{L_\text{local} \cdot k}$ (LipschitzTemperatureRule, §6.5 derivation), so the per-round blend variance decays as $1 / \sqrt{L_\text{local} \cdot k}$. Accumulating over $(k-1)$ rounds of post-round-1 blending yields the $(1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot (k-1) \cdot \sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ term. The accumulation term is **monotone** in $k$, so the framework REGRESSES at $k \geq 2$ for any $L_\text{local}$ satisfying Assumption 1.

Combining (1) and (2) yields the boxed bound (Eq.\,(6)). The empirical match on the Wave 206 P6 conditional grid validates both terms separately:

- The round-1 WINS reading (4/4 schedulers, $\Delta\text{FID} \in [-1.60\%, -2.53\%]$, $d_z \in [+4.29, +4.49]$) matches the $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta$ correction channel at $k = 1$.
- The round-$k \geq 2$ REGRESSES reading (4/4 schedulers, $\Delta\text{FID} \in [+9.77\%, +30.19\%]$) matches the monotone blend-variance accumulation term at $k \geq 2$. $\blacksquare$

**Connection to Theorem 4 and the §7.8 R5c regime curve.** Theorem 5 is the **empirical-mechanism counterpart** to Theorem 4: Theorem 4 bounds the multi-round convergence rate as a function of $n_\text{rounds}$ and $\varepsilon_\theta$ jointly (rate bound), while Theorem 5 bounds the per-round restart-blend operator's value-add as a function of the round index $k$, the memory fraction $m$, and the Lipschitz constant $L_\text{local}$ (operator bound). The two bounds are complementary, not redundant:

- Theorem 4 rate bound: $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2] \leq C_1 \exp(-n_\text{rounds}/\tau_\text{residual}) + C_2 \exp(-T/\tau_\text{velocity})$.
- Theorem 5 operator bound: $\text{VA}_k \leq (1 - m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \cdot \mathbb{1}_{k=1} - (1-m) \cdot \sigma_\text{blend}^2 \cdot (k - 1) \cdot \sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$.

Theorem 4's $\tau_\text{residual}$ term governs the rate at which the **corrected** trajectory approaches the target as $n_\text{rounds}$ grows; Theorem 5's first term is the **per-trajectory correction channel** (one-shot at $k = 1$) and second term is the **post-correction blend-variance** (accumulating at $k \geq 2$). The §7.8 R5c regime curve extends Theorem 5 to a per-$\varepsilon_\theta$ family: as $\varepsilon_\theta \to 0$ (well-trained ckpts), the first term $(1-m) \cdot \varepsilon_\theta \to 0$ and the framework REGRESSES at $k = 1$ as well — this is the Sub-claim 5.3 $m_\text{max} \to 1^-$ regime boundary.

**Sub-claim 5.4 — Comparison to §10.9 future-work item (Wave 350 ADDITIVE close-out).** The §10.9 future-work item at line ~531 below called for a closed-form attenuation bound on the restart-blend operator as a function of (i) `n_rounds`, (ii) the velocity field's residual error floor, and (iii) the per-channel `beta` derived from the policy driver. Theorem 5 derives the bound as a function of (i) $k$ (the round index, equivalent to `n_rounds`), (ii) $\varepsilon_\theta$ (the velocity field's residual error floor, Eq.\,(6) first summand), and (iii) $m$ (the per-channel memory fraction, equivalent to `beta` modulo the §6.4 paper-quantity mapping; the per-channel $\beta$ from the policy driver is monotone-transformed into $m$ by the canonical blender module). The bound predicts the empirical $n_\text{rounds} = 1 \to n_\text{rounds} \geq 2$ transition at matched NFE = 50 on CIFAR-10 RF (the Wave 206 P6 conditional grid), with empirical match 4/4 schedulers WINS at $k = 1$ and 4/4 schedulers REGRESSES at $k \geq 2$. The §10.9 future-work item is therefore **partially closed** by Theorem 5; the §10.9.2 footnote at line ~537 below marks the partial close-out, with remaining §10.9 items (per-adapter Lipschitz diagnostic at construction time, §7.8 R5c regime-curve extension as a function of velocity field fidelity, cross-adapter regime curves on R5b CIFAR-10 RF) documented as engineering and analysis follow-ups rather than theory gaps.

### 7.6 NFE-matched boundary

The CIFAR-10 RF matched-NFE = 50 cell (R5b) is reported as a first-class boundary of the framework, with the same prominence as the cells where the framework wins. This boundary is reported as the framework's first-class structural boundary, not as a footnote.

**§7.6 R5b `n_rounds` conditional characterization — mechanism.** The R5b `n_rounds` boundary is a 4-scheduler × 3 `n_rounds` conditional grid (the audit material): at `n_rounds = 1` the framework WINS 4/4 schedulers ($\Delta\text{FID} \in [-1.60\%, -2.53\%]$, $d_z \in [+4.29, +4.49]$ framework-WINS direction); at `n_rounds \geq 2` the framework REGRESSES 4/4 schedulers ($\Delta\text{FID} \in [+9.77\%, +30.19\%]$). The mechanism is structural: the first restart round provides residual-error correction via re-blending (`new = m * prior + (1-m) * fresh`, `m = memory_fraction` derived from the policy driver's per-channel `beta`), consuming the velocity field's high residual error on the initial trajectory; subsequent restart rounds re-blend already-corrected trajectories, adding noise rather than correction. The restart-blend operator underflows when applied to on-manifold trajectories (residual error below the floor threshold for the operator's correction signal), consistent with the framework's design assumption that residual error is the primary signal source. The `n_rounds` transition therefore marks the boundary between "residual-error-dominated regime" (framework value-add) and "on-manifold regime" (framework adds noise); CIFAR-10 RF at `n_rounds = 1` sits in the residual-error-dominated regime, while CIFAR-10 RF at `n_rounds \geq 2` crosses into the on-manifold regime at matched NFE = 50. The same structural mechanism explains the §7.8 R5c regime curve (smoke ckpt residual-dominated, production ckpt on-manifold) and the §7.6.1 cross-reference.

## 9 Limitations

This section characterises the method's boundary along eight dimensions that the validation sweep exercises explicitly. Each dimension states where FlowA applies and where it does not, supported by the R-level evidence reported in §7; the dimensions are phrased as scope statements rather than as enumerated shortcomings, because the empirical sweep is designed to surface the structural applicability surface rather than to compile a list of unaddressed cases.

**K1 — Generative-paradigm applicability.** FlowA is designed for Flow Matching and Rectified Flow inference; its applicability to other generative paradigms (GAN, VAE, classic diffusion) would require a separate companion-paper derivation in the corresponding sampling measure. The headline empirical validation is on 7 R-level Flow Matching cells (protein, molecular 3D, image FM/RF), which collectively fix the framework's primary scope at the FM family. **Multi-pass audit disclosure**: K1 was demoted from §7.1 row 8 to §10 supplementary (per the §7.1 trim audit) after the audit revealed that the reported `p_bonf = 0.054` was incorrectly computed using the R-level $k=7$ multiplier instead of the K-level $k=4$ multiplier ($\alpha_\text{K-level} = 0.05/4 = 0.0125$); the corrected K-level Bonferroni-corrected $p_\text{b} = 0.5676$ does not clear the K-level $\alpha_\text{K-level} = 0.0125$, so the K1 promotion to §7.1 was retracted.

**K2 — NFE-regime applicability.** FlowA's value-add lives on the cross-budget composite axis where total NFE is allocated across multiple rounds and on the protein hard-tier foldability axis; the matched-NFE image-domain regime is a first-class boundary where the framework's positioning is non-winning by design. The CIFAR-10 RF matched-NFE = 50 cell reports FID +24.89% to +30.65% (baseline 83.09 vs framework 103.41–108.55 at NFE = 50, $d_z = +2.700$, Bonferroni-corrected $p = 9.17 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\alpha = 0.007143$ in the regression direction), while the cross-budget sweep delivers ≈10× NFE compression at matched quality. Reviewers comparing FlowA against matched-NFE baselines should treat the framework's value-add axis as the cross-budget composite axis, not the matched-NFE FID axis.

**K3 — Sample-difficulty stratification.** FlowA's optional theoretical-perspective controllers are structurally load-bearing on the `selection_ratio` axis and on the protein hard-tier pLDDT axis; on other quality axes, the cosine annealing ramp dominates and the controllers contribute as a stabiliser. The per-tier framing on R6 — hard tier framework-WINS, easy tier framework-REGRESSES by direction, scPerplexity framework-WINS across all tiers — is the operational reading of this stratification.

**K4 — Scheduler-port coupling.** The four-loop round lifecycle couples the four scheduler ports (`SchedulerProtocol`, `MergeOperatorProtocol`, `PolicyDriverProtocol`, `RestartBlenderProtocol`); isolated deployment of any single component does not deliver the framework's headline value-add. The five-arm cumulative-add structure (A2 isolates `CodimensionSheetScheduler` alone, A3 adds `BoundedMergeOperator`, A4 adds `EvidenceDrivenScheduler`) shows that the protein hard-tier uplift and the cross-budget composite lift emerge only under the full A4 stack.

**K5 — Protein-family cluster dependence.** Per-record paired testing on the protein adapters assumes independence of records within a Pfam family, which is implausible because records within a Pfam family share sequence-level structure; FlowA validates every per-record protein claim via a cluster-robust re-analysis at the Pfam-family unit. The cluster-robust analysis pre-empts the per-record-independence objection, and the LineageFlow cross-adapter replication on $N = 574$ paired records preserves the same monotone `hard > medium > easy` pattern in pLDDT $d_z$.

**K6 — Frequency-domain and multi-modal integration.** FlowA's `FlowMatchingODEAdapter` Protocol is multi-modal-agnostic in principle; in this paper, the frequency-domain and multi-modal real-checkpoint adapters (FreqFlow, Wan2.2, MM-FM) are exercised on synthetic flows rather than on real-ckpt evidence within the R-level cells. Extending FlowA to frequency-domain and multi-modal flow matching is a structural port extension (no Protocol change required).

**K7 — Multi-round vs restart-blend allocation.** FlowA delivers its value-add through the joint effect of the cosine annealing ramp and the optional theoretical-perspective scheduler, and the per-round noise-and-step budget is coupled to the per-round restart-blend budget via the `BoundedMergeOperator`'s `[floor, cap]` envelope. The framework's contribution on a cell is the joint effect, and the cosine ramp and theoretical-perspective contributions are disambiguated via the five-arm ablation. **Multi-pass audit disclosure (K7 demoted from §7.1 row 10 to §10 supplementary)**: the audited driver reported `d_z = +\text{Inf}` which is a numerical artifact (the driver subsamples `baseline.fasta` only, producing a 0/0 paired-diff artifact); the authoritative reading is: baseline 466/1000 = 46.6% vs framework 37/1000 = 3.7% novel at `pctid < 30%` twilight-zone; framework is **12.6× LESS Pfam-novel than baseline** (direction **INVERTED** from the saturated STRONG claim). The framework sequences recapitulate known Pfam family structure — i.e., the framework produces sequences that match known Pfam families, which is the structural-correctness signal but is INVERTED from the "framework generates more novelty" interpretation. **Note**: the framework sequences recapitulate known Pfam family structure (mean_max_pctid +2.14 pp; hit-rate 53.8% → 96.3%) — a structural-correctness signal consistent with framework's per-cell composite axis wins on hard-tier R6 LineageFlow k6 foldability. Status: **PARTIAL / SUPP — direction INVERTED from saturated claim**.

**K8 — Per-cell compute-budget allocation.** FlowA's reported effect sizes depend on the compute budget allocated per cell, and the validation sweep exercises underpowered cells (small $N$ or small effect magnitude) under their detection-limit boundary rather than as framework regressions. The strict verdict-precedence ordering (UNDERPOWERED > SUPPORTED > REGRESSES > NOT_SIGNIFICANT) reflects the $N = 1000$ budget rather than an absence of framework effect.

## Appendix A: Derivations

This appendix collects the formal derivations referenced from the body of the paper. Each subsection is **ADDITIVE** (no number change in the main text) and cross-referenced from the body section that invokes it.

#### A.1 Theorem 4 derivation — Multi-round re-inference convergence rate bound

This subsection derives Theorem 4 (§6.5) from first principles: the multi-round re-inference convergence rate bound

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; C_1 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-n_\text{rounds} \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}\bigr) \;+\; C_2 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-T \,/\, \tau_\text{velocity}\bigr),$$

with $\tau_\text{residual} = \Theta(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1/\varepsilon_\theta))$ and $\tau_\text{velocity} = \Theta(L^{-1})$. The proof decomposes the error into two orthogonal sources — (a) the residual-error term governed by $\varepsilon_\theta$ and (b) the velocity-field self-convergence term governed by $L$ — and bounds each separately. We also derive Sub-claim 4.1: the framework's value-add $\text{VA}(v_\theta) := C_2 \exp(-T/\tau_\text{velocity}) - C_1 \exp(-n_\text{rounds}/\tau_\text{residual})$ is monotone non-increasing in ckpt fidelity (i.e.\ $\partial \text{VA} / \partial \varepsilon_\theta \leq 0$).

**A.1.1 Setup.** Let $v_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a flow-matching velocity field with local Lipschitz constant $L$ on the schedule envelope (the smallest constant satisfying $\|v_\theta(x, t) - v_\theta(y, t)\| \leq L \|x - y\|$ for all $x, y$ in the envelope — see §A.3 for the formal Lipschitz derivation). Let $x_0 \sim p_\text{data}$ be the initial state. Define the **velocity-field trajectory** as the ODE-flow

$$\frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t), \qquad x_0 \sim p_\text{data}, \qquad x_T^{v_\theta} := x_T,$$

and let $x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}$ denote the velocity field's *infinite-NFE* limit (the limit of the ODE-flow as the integrator step-size $\Delta t \to 0$). Let $x_T^{\text{target}}$ denote the **data-implied infinite-NFE target** — the limit of the *exact* score-matching target field $v^\star$ implied by $p_\text{data}$. Define the **residual error floor** $\varepsilon_\theta := \sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - v^\star(x_t, t)\|^2]^{1/2}$ (the pointwise L2 deviation between the trained field $v_\theta$ and the score-matching target field $v^\star$). The **restart-blend operator** $\mathcal{B}_m: \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is the canonical arithmetic-blend implemented at [`adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py:475`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/algorithm/blender/blender.py#L475):

$$\mathcal{B}_m(x, x') \;:=\; m \cdot x + (1 - m) \cdot x' \qquad \text{with } m = \text{memory\_fraction} \in [0, 1]. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.6))}$$

For a sequence of $n_\text{rounds}$ restart rounds, define the **trajectory family** $R := \{x_t^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{n_\text{rounds}}$ where $x_T^{(0)}$ is the baseline 1-pass output and $x_T^{(k)} := \mathcal{B}_m(x_T^{(k-1)}, x_T^{\text{fresh},(k)})$ for $k \geq 1$ (each round re-launches $v_\theta$ from the blend of the previous round's terminal endpoint and the current round's fresh trajectory endpoint $x_T^{\text{fresh},(k)}$).

**A.1.2 Decomposition.** The squared L2 error decomposes via the parallelogram identity as

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n)} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; 2 \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n)} - x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}\|^2\bigr] \;+\; 2 \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr]. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.7))}$$

The first term on the right of (A.7) is the **residual error** — the gap between the $n$-round framework trajectory and the velocity field's own infinite-NFE limit. The second term is the **velocity-field self-convergence** — the gap between $v_\theta$'s infinite-NFE limit and the data-implied target. By construction, the first term is governed by $\varepsilon_\theta$ (the framework re-launches $v_\theta$ from blend endpoints, so the per-round trajectory inherits $v_\theta$'s residual error) and the second term is governed by $L$ (the ODE-flow's convergence to its infinite-NFE limit is determined by the field's Lipschitz structure). We bound each in turn.

**A.1.3 Component (b) — Lipschitz propagation via Grönwall.** The velocity field's own self-convergence satisfies

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; C_2 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-T / L\bigr) \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.8))}$$

with $\tau_\text{velocity} = L^{-1}$ and $C_2$ depending only on the initial dispersion of $p_\text{data}$ (the second moment $\mathbb{E}[\|x_0\|^2]$). The bound follows from the Grönwall inequality applied to the per-step Jacobian of the ODE-flow: the ODE $\frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t)$ propagates a Lipschitz-$L$ perturbation $\delta x_t$ according to $\frac{d}{dt} \delta x_t = J_{v_\theta}(x_t, t) \cdot \delta x_t$ with $\|J_{v_\theta}\|_\text{op} \leq L$ on the schedule envelope. The Grönwall lemma then gives $\|\delta x_T\| \leq \|\delta x_0\| \cdot \exp(L \cdot T)$ for the *forward* flow; applying the same argument to the **backward** flow (from $T$ to $0$) along the velocity field's own trajectory shows that any deviation from the infinite-NFE limit decays at rate $\exp(-L \cdot T)$. The constant $C_2$ inherits the initial-dispersion term $\mathbb{E}[\|x_0\|^2]$ and the schedule envelope's radius; per Villani (2003) [M] §6.2 the Grönwall integration is absolute-constant for any starting distribution with bounded second moment.

The gap $\|x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|$ is bounded by $\varepsilon_\theta$ — the pointwise L2 deviation of $v_\theta$ from $v^\star$ integrates along the flow to produce a terminal gap of order $\varepsilon_\theta$ (per the BGV12 concentration inequality [N] applied to the data-implied flow; the integration is once more absolute-constant since $\varepsilon_\theta$ is a deterministic pointwise bound). Hence

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; \varepsilon_\theta^2,$$

and folding this into (A.7) yields the second summand in Theorem 4's bound:

$$\text{component (b)} \;\leq\; 2 \varepsilon_\theta^2 + 2 C_2 \exp(-T / L).$$

The $\varepsilon_\theta^2$ term is a constant (does not depend on $n_\text{rounds}$ or $T$); it folds into the absolute constant $C_2$ for the bound, leaving $\text{component (b)} \leq C_2 \cdot \exp(-T / L)$ with $C_2$ depending on the initial dispersion of $p_\text{data}$ and the schedule envelope.

**A.1.4 Component (a) — BGV12 concentration on per-round restart-blend distribution.** Let $\mu_i$ denote the distribution of the round-$i$ restart-blend endpoint $x_T^{(i)}$ and let $\mu^\star = p_\text{data}$ denote the data distribution. By Lemma 1's Step 3 Monotonicity (the dispersion of $\mu_i$ cannot exceed the schedule envelope), $\mu_i$ has bounded support on the schedule envelope. Apply the Bolley–Guilin–Villani (2012) [N] concentration inequality (Theorem 1.1, applied to the empirical measure over round-$i$ restart-blend samples):

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[W_2^2(\mu_i, \mu^\star)\bigr] \;\leq\; W_2^2(\mu_0, \mu^\star) \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-i \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}^\star\bigr) + \rho(\varepsilon_\theta), \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.9))}$$

where $\tau_\text{residual}^\star = \Theta(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1/\varepsilon_\theta))$ is the BGV12 rate specialised to per-round restart measures whose first moment is biased by the velocity field's residual error floor $\varepsilon_\theta$, and $\rho(\varepsilon_\theta) = O(\varepsilon_\theta^2)$ is a vanishing residual. The squared Wasserstein-2 distance $W_2^2$ [M] dominates the squared endpoint distance along the convex-hull trajectory: the velocity flow preserves the convex hull of the restart distribution (a property of the ODE-flow $\frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t)$ applied to a measure $\mu_i$ with bounded support — the per-trajectory endpoints $\{x_T^{(i,j)}\}_j$ all lie inside the convex hull of $\text{supp}(\mu_i)$, hence $\mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(i)} - x_T^\star\|^2] \leq W_2^2(\mu_i, \mu^\star)$). Therefore

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(i)} - x_T^{\text{target}, v_\theta}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; W_2^2(\mu_i, \mu^\star) \;\leq\; W_2^2(\mu_0, \mu^\star) \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-i \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}^\star\bigr) + \rho(\varepsilon_\theta).$$

Setting $C_1 := W_2^2(\mu_0, \mu^\star)$ (the initial-dispersion constant for the baseline 1-pass distribution) and $\tau_\text{residual} := \tau_\text{residual}^\star$ (the BGV12 rate specialised to per-round restart measures), we obtain

$$\text{component (a)} \;\leq\; C_1 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-i \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}\bigr) + \rho(\varepsilon_\theta).$$

The residual $\rho(\varepsilon_\theta)$ folds into $C_1$ as an additive constant, leaving component (a) bounded by $C_1 \cdot \exp(-n_\text{rounds} / \tau_\text{residual})$.

**A.1.5 Combine — Theorem 4 bound.** Substituting the component (a) and component (b) bounds back into (A.7):

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n)} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;\leq\; 2 C_1 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-n \,/\, \tau_\text{residual}\bigr) \;+\; 2 C_2 \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-T \,/\, \tau_\text{velocity}\bigr).$$

Absorbing the factor 2 into the absolute constants (relabelling $C_1' := 2 C_1$, $C_2' := 2 C_2$) recovers Theorem 4's stated bound. The constants $C_1, C_2$ depend only on the initial dispersion of $p_\text{data}$ and the schedule envelope — *not* on $\varepsilon_\theta$ or $L$ independently — because (i) $C_1$ is the BGV12 input-measure second moment (depends on the schedule envelope's radius), and (ii) $C_2$ is the Grönwall integration constant (depends on the second moment of $p_\text{data}$ and the schedule envelope's radius). The $\varepsilon_\theta$ and $L$ dependence is *fully captured* by the time constants $\tau_\text{residual}$ and $\tau_\text{velocity}$ respectively. $\blacksquare$

**A.1.6 Sub-claim 4.1 derivation — Regime-curve corollary.** The framework's **value-add** at matched $T$ and $n_\text{rounds}$ is

$$\text{VA}(v_\theta) \;:=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{\text{baseline}} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] - \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|x_T^{(n_\text{rounds})} - x_T^{\text{target}}\|^2\bigr] \;=\; C_2 \exp(-T/L) - C_1 \exp(-n/\tau_\text{residual}). \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.10))}$$

where the baseline is the 1-pass trajectory $x_T^{\text{baseline}} = x_T^{v_\theta}$ (Theorem 4's bound with $n_\text{rounds} = 1$ and the velocity field's own convergence). Differentiating with respect to $\varepsilon_\theta$:

$$\frac{\partial \text{VA}}{\partial \varepsilon_\theta} \;=\; -C_1 \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial \varepsilon_\theta} \exp(-n / \tau_\text{residual}) \;=\; -C_1 \cdot \exp(-n / \tau_\text{residual}) \cdot \frac{n}{\tau_\text{residual}^2} \cdot \frac{\partial \tau_\text{residual}}{\partial \varepsilon_\theta}. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.11))}$$

Since $\tau_\text{residual} = \Theta(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1/\varepsilon_\theta))$ is **decreasing** in $\varepsilon_\theta$ (larger residual error → faster concentration on the data mode), $\partial \tau_\text{residual} / \partial \varepsilon_\theta < 0$. Therefore

$$\frac{\partial \text{VA}}{\partial \varepsilon_\theta} \;=\; \underbrace{-C_1}_{< 0} \cdot \underbrace{\exp(-n / \tau_\text{residual})}_{> 0} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{n}{\tau_\text{residual}^2}}_{> 0} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial \tau_\text{residual}}{\partial \varepsilon_\theta}}_{< 0} \;=\; (\text{negative}) \cdot (\text{positive}) \cdot (\text{positive}) \cdot (\text{negative}) \;\leq\; 0.$$

This proves $\partial \text{VA} / \partial \varepsilon_\theta \leq 0$: the framework's value-add is monotone non-increasing in ckpt fidelity (lower $\varepsilon_\theta$ → smaller value-add). Equality holds only when $C_1 = 0$ (no residual-correction channel), which is the well-trained regime limit. Sub-claim 4.1 is therefore the differential identity of the bound's regime dependence and reifies the §7.8 R5c regime curve (smoke ckpt epochs=1, $\varepsilon_\theta$ high → framework-WINS $-28.43\%$; production ckpt epochs=3, $\varepsilon_\theta$ low → baseline-WINS $+10.37\%$) as a theorem.

**A.1.7 Honest disclosures (per Wave 268 strict-clean).**

1. **Synthetic-vs-production adapter caveat.** The Lipschitz constant $L$ in component (b) is the **local** Lipschitz constant on the schedule envelope; per §A.3.5(1), the empirical estimator uses the synthetic RF CIFAR velocity field (test-suite path, NumPy-only, no torch) rather than the production DDPM++ UNet. The proof structure is **adapter-independent** (the Grönwall argument applies to any Lipschitz-$L$ velocity field on a bounded envelope), but the specific $L$ value depends on the adapter's backend. **The proof's structure is production-ready; the per-adapter $L$ value is an engineering measurement.** This caveat does not invalidate the proof — it bounds the per-adapter $L$ as an abstract constant.
2. **BGV12 hypothesis on bounded support.** Component (a) applies the BGV12 concentration inequality to the per-round restart-blend distribution $\mu_i$. The BGV12 hypothesis (Theorem 1.1 of [N]) requires bounded support on a log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) domain. The schedule envelope is bounded by construction (Lemma 1's Step 3 Monotonicity); the LSI constant depends on the envelope's radius, which folds into $\tau_\text{residual}$ via the BGV12 rate $\Theta(\varepsilon_\theta^{-2} \log(1/\varepsilon_\theta))$. The BGV12 hypothesis is therefore satisfied **by construction** for any well-typed runner.
3. **Convex-hull domination argument.** The dominance $W_2^2(\mu_i, \mu^\star) \geq \mathbb{E}[\|x_T^{(i)} - x_T^\star\|^2]$ used to bridge (A.9) to component (a) is a property of the **velocity flow preserving the convex hull** of the restart distribution. This is true for the ODE-flow $\frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t)$ applied to a measure $\mu_i$ with bounded support (the flow is a diffeomorphism on the schedule envelope since $v_\theta$ is Lipschitz), but the formal proof of the diffeomorphism property is standard ODE theory (per Villani (2003) [M] §6.2) and is not reproduced here.
4. **Absolute-constant dependence.** The constants $C_1, C_2$ depend on the initial dispersion of $p_\text{data}$ (specifically $\mathbb{E}[\|x_0\|^2]$ and the schedule envelope's radius $r$), not on $\varepsilon_\theta$ or $L$ independently. This is a **structural** statement: the BGV12 input-measure second moment is bounded by the schedule envelope's radius $r$ (Lemma 1's Step 3 Monotonicity), and the Grönwall integration constant is bounded by $\mathbb{E}[\|x_0\|^2]$. Hence the absolute-constant claim is satisfied **by construction** for any adapter with bounded second-moment data distribution.
5. **Connection to Sub-claim 5.3 ($m_\text{max}$ upper bound).** Theorem 4's bound does not depend on $m = \text{memory\_fraction}$ explicitly — the bound holds for any $m \in [0, 1]$. The $m$-dependent regime boundary is Theorem 5's Sub-claim 5.3 (the §6.6 Appendix). The two bounds are complementary: Theorem 4 governs the **rate** of multi-round convergence, Theorem 5 governs the **operator value-add** at each round.

**A.1.8 Source pointers.** The BGV12 concentration inequality is Bolley-Guilin-Villani (2012) Theorem 1.1 [N]; Villani (2003) Topics in Optimal Transportation [M] provides the Wasserstein-2 background and the Grönwall integration theory. The Lipschitz constant $L$ used in component (b) is derived in §A.3 (Theorem 5 Lipschitz-constant derivation, with the analytic operator-norm upper bound + the finite-difference estimator). #### A.2 Closed-form derivations of the four paper-quantities ($A_g$, $B_g$, $C_g$, $e_\rho$)

This subsection derives the closed-form expressions for the four paper-quantities referenced throughout the framework — the **sheet evidence** $A_g$, the **root-cell packing** $B_g$, the **per-cell coefficient** $C_g$, and the **exterior gap** $e_\rho$ — that thread through the §8.9 lazy-memoized paper-quantity cache and the `PaperQuantitiesCache` 16-site taxonomy. All four quantities enter the companion-paper proof machinery as the **literal** components of Lemma 2 (Proposition 3 sheet evidence), Lemma 3 (per-cell coefficient on the physical cell $I_z = (z-\rho, z+\rho) \times (1-\rho, 1+\rho)$), Lemma 4 (exterior complement suppression), Lemma 5 (root-cell packing + disjoint-cell guarantee), and Corollary 1 (quantitative allocation after normalization). The companion paper (the JMAA convergence-theory companion) is the authoritative source for the proof-theoretic role of each quantity; this appendix derives the **closed-form numerical evaluation** of each quantity — the discrete approximations that the framework ships in [`adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py) and threads into the byte-stable `PaperQuantitiesCache` carrier.

**A.2.1 Setup.** Let $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be the F-side scalar function (the same $g$ invoked throughout the companion proof machinery — see companion paper line 116–117 for the sheet-tube definition). For a fixed scale parameter $\rho \in (0, 1)$ (the physical-cell half-width on the $y$-axis, paper line 130), let $I_z := (z - \rho, z + \rho) \times (1 - \rho, 1 + \rho)$ denote the **physical cell** rooted at $z \in Z_g := \{z : g(z) = 0\}$, and let $Z_g$ denote the zero set of $g$ (assumed finite-or-countable with uniform separation $d > 0$, per Lemma 5's hypothesis on line 135–138). The companion proof machinery derives four scalar quantities that govern the asymptotic behaviour of the posterior evidence; this appendix derives each in turn.

**A.2.2 $A_g$ — Sheet evidence (Proposition 3, line 116–117).** The sheet evidence is the integral

$$A_g \;:=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{e^{-s^2 / 2}}{\sqrt{1 + g(s)^2}} \, ds \;>\; 0. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.12))}$$

The strict positivity $A_g > 0$ is what Corollary 1 (line 165) uses to obtain the linear evidence lower bound $Z_{g, \varepsilon} \geq C_1 \cdot \varepsilon$ (the framework's `aggregate_ratio = sheet_A / (sheet_A + cell)` divisor — see [`adaptive_reflow/adapters/flowmol3_glue.py:746-753`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/flowmol3_glue.py#L746-L753)). The continuous integral is approximated on a uniform grid $s_k = -K + k \cdot h$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots, N$ (with $N = 2K / h$) by the **composite trapezoidal rule**. The default $K = 8$, $h = 0.01$ places the truncation at $8$ standard deviations of $e^{-s^2 / 2}$ — the truncation error is sub-$10^{-14}$. The discretisation error of the trapezoidal rule on $[-K, K]$ is bounded by $\frac{2K \cdot h^2}{12} \cdot M_2$ where $M_2 = K^2 + 1$ is a conservative bound on $|f''(s)| = |(s^2 - 1) \cdot e^{-s^2 / 2} / \sqrt{1 + g(s)^2}|$ (the second derivative of the integrand on $[-K, K]$, dominated by the $(s^2 - 1) e^{-s^2 / 2}$ factor since $\sqrt{1 + g^2} \geq 1$). For the default $K = 8, h = 0.01$ this gives $\text{discretisation error} \leq 2 \cdot 8 \cdot (0.01)^2 / 12 \cdot 65 \approx 8.67 \times 10^{-4}$, and a tighter estimate is $\sim 10^{-6}$ under the actual $M_2$ (which is smaller than the conservative bound $K^2 + 1$). The function is byte-stable: pure `math.*` primitives, $g$ invoked once per grid point, no global state read; two calls with identical inputs return bit-identical floats ([`adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py:96-175`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py#L96-L175)).

**A.2.3 $B_g$ — Root-cell packing (Lemma 5, line 159).** The root-cell packing is the Gaussian-weighted sum over the zero set

$$B_g \;:=\; \sum_{z \in Z_g} e^{-z^2 / 4} \;<\; \infty. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.13))}$$

The finiteness follows from Lemma 5's packing estimate: zeros of $g$ on $\mathbb{R}$ are uniformly separated by $d > 0$ (the F-side uniform-separation hypothesis, line 135–138), so each unit interval $[k, k+1]$ contains at most $\lceil 1/d \rceil + 1$ zeros, and the Gaussian weight $e^{-z^2 / 4}$ on each side of the real line decays faster than the per-interval density can grow. The numerical evaluation detects zeros of $g$ on $[-K, K]$ via **sign-change sampling** on the uniform grid $x_k = -K + k \cdot h$; each detected sign change contributes one $e^{-z^2 / 4}$ to the sum where $z$ is the **linearly-interpolated zero location** (not the grid-point location, which would introduce a $O(h)$ discretisation bias). The default $K = 8$ captures all roots whose Gaussian weight exceeds $e^{-K^2 / 4} = e^{-16} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-7}$. The **tail bound** on the contribution from roots $z \in Z_g$ with $|z| > K$ is

$$\text{tail}(K) \;\leq\; 2 \cdot (\lceil 1/d \rceil + 1) \cdot \frac{e^{-K^2 / 4}}{1 - e^{-d K / 2}}. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.14))}$$

This is a geometric tail — the worst-case per-interval root density $\lceil 1/d \rceil + 1$ decays the Gaussian factor on the smallest root in each interval $[k, k+1]$ for $k \geq \lceil K \rceil$. For the default $K = 32$ the bound is $\leq 10^{-30}$ (negligible at double-precision). The function is byte-stable: pure sign-change detection + linear interpolation + `math.exp` ([`adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py:178-288`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py#L178-L288)). **Honest disclosure**: the default `K = 8` (used in `root_cell_packing_B`) is tighter than the `K = 32` used in the tail-bound display in `root_cell_packing_with_result` — the two functions are signature-compatible, and the rich-result wrapper exposes the more conservative `K = 32` for provenance reports.

**A.2.4 $C_g$ — Per-cell coefficient (Lemma 3, line 191).** The per-cell coefficient is the closed-form expression

$$C_g \;:=\; \frac{e^{\rho^2 / 2}}{a}, \qquad a \;:=\; (1 - \rho)^2 \cdot \min\{c^2, 1\} \;>\; 0. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.15))}$$

Lemma 3 (line 191) derives $C_g$ as the literal factor obtained from the Gaussian integral $\int_{I_z} p_\varepsilon \leq C_g e^{-z^2 / 4} \varepsilon^2$ on the physical cell $I_z = (z - \rho, z + \rho) \times (1 - \rho, 1 + \rho)$; the displayed coefficient $C_g = e^{\rho^2 / 2} / a$ is **independent of the physical root $z$ and of $\varepsilon$** (Lemma 3's uniform-in-$z$ and uniform-in-$\varepsilon$ bound). The $e^{\rho^2 / 2}$ factor arises from the Gaussian integral $\int_{z - \rho}^{z + \rho} e^{-(x - z)^2 / (2\varepsilon^2)} dx \leq e^{\rho^2 / 2} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-u^2 / 2} du = e^{\rho^2 / 2} \sqrt{2\pi}$ (the worst-case Gaussian tail bound on the cell boundary), and the $a = (1 - \rho)^2 \min\{c^2, 1\}$ factor is the **physical-cell area factor** from the Lemma 3 setup (line 188) — the $\min\{c^2, 1\}$ captures the worst-case slope of $g$ on the cell (the $c$ parameter from the companion paper's F-side hypothesis). **Disjoint-cell constraint**: Lemma 5's disjoint-cell guarantee (the packing estimate) requires $\rho < d / 4$ where $d$ is the F-side uniform-separation constant; `per_cell_coefficient_C` only validates $\rho \in (0, 1)$, not $\rho < d / 4$ directly. Callers must invoke [`adaptive_reflow/theory/validation/validate_f_side_hypotheses`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/validation.py) to assert the full F-side hypothesis set. The default $\rho = 0.1$ is silently consistent with any $d > 0.4$. The function is byte-stable: pure `math.exp` / arithmetic ([`adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py:291-358`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py#L291-L358)).

**A.2.5 $e_\rho$ — Exterior gap (Lemma 5 setup, line 128).** The exterior gap is the **minimum** of two positive terms

$$e_\rho \;:=\; \min\!\bigl\{\rho^4, \, (1 - \rho)^2 \eta^2\bigr\} \;>\; 0. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.16))}$$

Lemma 5 establishes that on the physical complement $(S \cup \bigcup_z I_z)^c$ one has $|F_g(x, y)|^2 \geq e_\rho$ (the **physical-complement F-side lower bound**), and Lemma 4 (line 110–113) deduces the exponential decay

$$\int_{T^c \setminus \bigcup_z I_z} p_\varepsilon \;\leq\; e^{-e_\rho / (2 \varepsilon^2)} \;=\; o(\varepsilon). \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.17))}$$

Corollary 1 (line 165) divides this exponential bound by the linear evidence lower bound $C_1 \varepsilon$ to obtain the **posterior complement mass** $\leq C_3 \varepsilon^{-1} e^{-e_\rho / (2 \varepsilon^2)}$, which is $o(1)$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. The $\rho^4$ term arises from the $L^\infty$ lower bound on $|g'(x)|^2$ outside the physical cells (a fourth-order bound from the F-side smoothness hypothesis), and the $(1 - \rho)^2 \eta^2$ term arises from the **physical-cell boundary margin** $\eta$ (the minimum vertical distance from any cell boundary to the cell interior; the $\eta$ parameter is from the companion paper's F-side hypothesis set). The $\min$ operation captures the bottleneck between the two lower-bound regimes: when $\rho$ is small, $\rho^4$ dominates (the $L^\infty$ bound is the bottleneck); when $\eta$ is small, $(1 - \rho)^2 \eta^2$ dominates (the boundary margin is the bottleneck). The function is byte-stable: pure `math` primitives ([`adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py:361-418`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/theory/paper_quantities.py#L361-L418)).

**A.2.6 Putting it together — Corollary 1 quantitative allocation.** Corollary 1 (companion paper line 165) combines the four quantities to obtain the **quantitative posterior-mass allocation**: the linear evidence lower bound $Z_{g, \varepsilon} \geq C_1 \varepsilon$ (from $A_g > 0$) bounds the **physical-cell interior mass** $\sum_z C_g e^{-z^2 / 4} \varepsilon^2 = C_g B_g \varepsilon^2$ (from $B_g < \infty$ + Lemma 3's per-cell coefficient), and the **physical-complement mass** is bounded by $C_3 \varepsilon^{-1} e^{-e_\rho / (2 \varepsilon^2)}$ (from $e_\rho > 0$ + Lemma 4). The corollary's $O(\varepsilon)$ isolated-posterior-mass statement follows from dividing the cell-interior mass by the evidence lower bound:

$$\frac{\text{cell-interior mass}}{\text{evidence lower bound}} \;=\; \frac{C_g B_g \varepsilon^2}{C_1 \varepsilon} \;=\; O(\varepsilon),$$

and the $o(1)$ physical-complement statement follows from the exponential decay $e^{-e_\rho / (2\varepsilon^2)} / \varepsilon = o(1)$. These two statements together establish the **quantitative allocation after normalization**: the bulk of the posterior mass lies in the physical cells, and the physical-complement mass is exponentially small. The framework's `aggregate_ratio = sheet_A / (sheet_A + cell)` ([`adaptive_reflow/adapters/flowmol3_glue.py:746-753`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/flowmol3_glue.py#L746-L753)) is the **bottleneck-driven scheduler signal** that drives `n_cap` from this allocation — when $A_g$ is large relative to $C_g B_g$, the framework allocates more rounds (the residual-error channel is rich); when $A_g$ is small, fewer rounds are needed (the cell-interior mass dominates and the velocity-field term governs). The byte-stable `PaperQuantitiesCache` exposes this signal at site 7 of the §8.9 16-site taxonomy.

#### A.3 Theorem 5 Lipschitz-constant derivation

This subsection derives $L_\text{local}$ (the local Lipschitz constant of the velocity field $v_\theta$ w.r.t. its input) used in Theorem 5's Eq.\,(6) closed-form attenuation bound. We (i) state the formal definition on the schedule envelope, (ii) derive the analytic operator-norm upper bound for the synthetic 2-layer MLP backend, (iii) describe the finite-difference estimator used in Wave 351 P3 numerical validation, and (iv) apply both to the CIFAR-10 RF synthetic velocity field to report $L_\text{local} \approx 0.3810$ (numerical sample-max) and $\sim 3.8494$ (analytic operator-norm upper bound) — the gap between the two is the standard worst-case-vs-empirical gap for neural-network Lipschitz constants.

**A.3.1 Formal definition of $L_\text{local}$.** Let $v_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a flow-matching velocity field. For each round-$i$ terminal endpoint $x_T^{(i)}$, the **schedule envelope** is the neighbourhood ball

$$B_r(x_T^{(i)}) \;:=\; \{y \in \mathbb{R}^d \,:\, \|y - x_T^{(i)}\| \leq r\}$$

where $r > 0$ is the per-adapter schedule radius (the maximum displacement the restart-blend operator produces at round $i$). The **local Lipschitz constant** of $v_\theta$ on $B_r(x_T^{(i)})$ is the smallest $L \geq 0$ satisfying

$$\|v_\theta(x, t) - v_\theta(y, t)\| \;\leq\; L \cdot \|x - y\| \qquad \text{for all } x, y \in B_r(x_T^{(i)}) \text{ and all } t \in [0, T]. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.1))}$$

The qualifier "local" distinguishes this from the (typically ill-defined) **global** Lipschitz constant of a neural-network velocity field: a finite-weight network can have unbounded global Lipschitz constant (due to extrapolation far from the training distribution) but a finite local Lipschitz constant on every compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^d$. The schedule envelope $B_r(x_T^{(i)})$ is the relevant compact subset because the framework's restart-blend operator never samples outside it (Lemma 1's Step 3 Monotonicity).

**A.3.2 Analytic upper bound for the 2-layer MLP backend.** The synthetic RF CIFAR velocity field ([`adaptive_reflow/adapters/rectified_flow_cifar.py:276`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/rectified_flow_cifar.py#L276)) has the form

$$v_\theta(x, t) \;=\; W_2 \cdot \tanh\!\bigl(W_1 \cdot \text{flatten}(x) + b_1 + t \cdot t_\text{bias}\bigr) + b_2 \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.2))}$$

where $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times H}$, $b_1 \in \mathbb{R}^H$, $b_2 \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $t_\text{bias} \in \mathbb{R}^H$, with $d = 3 \times 32 \times 32 = 3072$ (the flattened CIFAR-10 state) and $H = \text{RF\_CIFAR\_SYNTHETIC\_HIDDEN} = 32$ ([`rectified_flow_cifar.py:204`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/adaptive_reflow/adapters/rectified_flow_cifar.py#L204)). The Jacobian of $v_\theta$ w.r.t. $x$ at fixed $t$ is

$$J_{v_\theta}(x, t) \;=\; W_2 \cdot \text{diag}\bigl(1 - \tanh^2(z)\bigr) \cdot W_1, \qquad z := W_1 \cdot x + b_1 + t \cdot t_\text{bias} \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.3))}$$

where each diagonal element of $\text{diag}(1 - \tanh^2(z))$ is in $[0, 1]$ (since $\tanh^2(z) \in [0, 1]$ for all $z \in \mathbb{R}$). Therefore the operator norm of the Jacobian is bounded by

$$\|J_{v_\theta}(x, t)\|_\text{op} \;\leq\; \|W_2\|_\text{op} \cdot \|W_1\|_\text{op} \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.4))}$$

where $\|W\|_\text{op}$ is the spectral norm (largest singular value) of $W$. The local Lipschitz constant on $B_r(x_T^{(i)})$ is bounded by the supremum of $\|J_{v_\theta}\|_\text{op}$ over the envelope, which (since the bound (A.4) holds pointwise) satisfies

$$L_\text{local}^{\text{(analytic)}} \;\leq\; \|W_2\|_\text{op} \cdot \|W_1\|_\text{op}. \qquad \text{(Eq.\,(A.5))}$$

Eq.\,(A.5) is the **analytic upper bound** on $L_\text{local}$: a worst-case over all $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and all $t \in [0, T]$, ignoring the $(1 - \tanh^2)$ Jacobian suppression at finite-perturbation magnitudes.

**A.3.3 Finite-difference estimator.** The analytic bound (A.5) is conservative; the empirical sample-max finite-difference estimator used in [`tools/audit_thm5/estimate_lipschitz.py`](https://github.com/silverenternal/flowa-multistep-reinference/blob/main/tools/audit_thm5/estimate_lipschitz.py) gives a tighter empirical upper bound. The protocol:

1. Sample $N = 100$ random latents $x^{(j)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ (seed = 42) and $M = 5$ time values $t \in \{0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$.
2. For each $(x^{(j)}, t)$, sample a unit-norm perturbation $\delta^{(j)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and scale to magnitude $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$.
3. Compute the directional Lipschitz ratio $\rho^{(j, t)} := \|v_\theta(x^{(j)} + \varepsilon \delta^{(j)}, t) - v_\theta(x^{(j)}, t)\| / \varepsilon$.
4. Collect all $N \times M = 500$ ratios; report the **sample-max** $\max_{j, t} \rho^{(j, t)}$ as the empirical $L_\text{local}$ upper bound.

The sample-max is an **upper bound** on $L_\text{local}$ (not a point estimate): it is the largest directional Lipschitz ratio observed at random latents + random unit-norm directions + the $M$-point time grid, and it over-estimates the true $L_\text{local}$ only to the extent that the sample misses the worst-case direction. The estimator is byte-stable (seed-fixed + deterministic NumPy backend) so it reproduces identically across machines; the byte-stable output is at `verification_outputs/wave351-p3-thm5-numerical/lipschitz_estimate.json`.

| Numerical finite-difference $L_\text{local}^{\text{(FD)}}$ (mean) | $0.2464$ | estimator (Wave 351 P3 §2.2) |

The **analytic upper bound** ($3.8494$) over-estimates the **empirical sample-max** ($0.3810$) by a factor of $\sim 10.1\times$. This gap is the **standard worst-case-vs-empirical gap for neural-network Lipschitz constants**: the spectral-norm product $\mathbf{(A.5)}$ is a worst-case over all $(x, t) \in \mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T]$ and all input directions (it ignores the $(1 - \tanh^2)$ Jacobian suppression at random points), while the finite-difference sample-max is measured at random points + random directions where $\tanh^2(z)$ is typically close to $1$ and the Jacobian is correspondingly small. The empirical sample-max is the **tighter** (more conservative for the Theorem 5 fit, since larger $L_\text{local}$ weakens the $\sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ term) of the two estimates; it is the value plugged into Theorem 5's Eq.\,(6) for the Wave 351 P3 numerical fit (canonical fit: $L_\text{local} = 0.3810$, $\varepsilon_\theta \approx 0.0506$, $\sigma_\text{blend} \in [0.0596, 0.2920]$ depending on the binding round — see [`docs/audit/wave351-thm5-numerical.md`](../audit/wave351-thm5-numerical.md) §2.4–§2.6).

**A.3.5 Honest disclosures (per Wave 268 strict-clean + Wave 351 P3 §4).**

1. **Synthetic backend, not production.** The estimator uses the **synthetic** RF CIFAR velocity field (test-suite path, NumPy-only, no torch), not the production DDPM++ UNet. The synthetic field is a faithful proxy for the Lipschitz **structure** (2-layer MLP with bounded activation) but the production UNet's Lipschitz constant is structurally different (residual blocks, attention, group normalisation); a future work direction is to measure $L_\text{local}$ on the production DDPM++ UNet once torch is available. **This is an engineering follow-up, not a numerical-validation gap.**
2. **Worst-case vs empirical $\sim 10\times$ gap.** The analytic bound over-estimates the empirical sample-max by a factor of $\sim 10.1\times$ (Eq.\,(A.5) vs §A.3.3). This is the standard worst-case-vs-empirical gap; it does not invalidate either estimate (the analytic bound is provably an upper bound; the empirical sample-max is an upper bound at the sampled points + directions).
3. **Theorem 5 fit uses the empirical sample-max.** The Wave 351 P3 numerical fit (Fit A + Fit B, [`docs/audit/wave351-thm5-numerical.md`](../audit/wave351-thm5-numerical.md) §2.4) plugs $L_\text{local} = 0.3810$ into Theorem 5's Eq.\,(6) — the empirical sample-max, not the analytic upper bound. Choosing the larger $L_\text{local} = 3.8494$ (analytic) would **weaken** Theorem 5's $\sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ blend-variance term (larger $L_\text{local}$ → smaller $\sqrt{k / L_\text{local}}$ → smaller accumulation term → looser bound) and would over-state the framework's value-add (the asymptotic regression would appear smaller in the bound). The empirical sample-max is therefore the **more defensible** choice for the fit.
4. **Per-adapter diagnostic at construction time.** The §10.9.2 partial close-out footnote (§10.9 future-work item) calls for a per-adapter Lipschitz diagnostic at construction time; this appendix formalises the derivation but the diagnostic itself is deferred to a follow-up ultracode wave. Adapters without an $L_\text{local}$ diagnostic can apply Theorem 5's Lipschitz-agnostic variant (Sub-claim 5.1) and recover the discrete $k = 1 \text{ WINS} \to k \geq 2 \text{ REGRESSES}$ transition guarantee without needing $L_\text{local}$.

## 3. Critical Wave 178 verification: kanzi NFE=100

The Wave 178 P6 N=10 sweep (`docs/audit/wave178-p6-e2e-eval.md`,
kanzi real ckpt, N=10 records per cell) showed framework pLDDT
**worse** than baseline at NFE=100 (52.83 vs 57.07, Δ = **−4.24**).
Wave 178 P7 then flagged this as plausibly noise because N=10 is
small and the framework-vs-baseline gap at NFE=50/200 was positive
for framework. This 3-seed × N=30 sweep is the multi-seed confirmation.

### 3.1 Per-seed kanzi NFE=100 numbers (from §2 raw data)

| seed | baseline pLDDT | framework pLDDT | Δ pLDDT (fw − bs) |
|-----:|---------------:|-----------------:|------------------:|
|   42 |          57.41 |            51.62 |          **−5.79** |
|   43 |          54.46 |            51.44 |          **−3.02** |
|   44 |          52.52 |            51.92 |          **−0.60** |
| mean |          54.80 |            51.66 |          **−3.13** |

| seed | baseline scPerp | framework scPerp | Δ scPerp (fw − bs) |
|-----:|----------------:|-----------------:|-------------------:|
|   42 |           19.50 |            16.48 |           **−3.02** |
|   43 |           18.88 |            15.85 |           **−3.03** |
|   44 |           20.25 |            15.53 |           **−4.72** |
| mean |           19.54 |            15.95 |           **−3.59** |

### 3.2 Verdict on Wave 178 NFE=100 "noise or real"

- **Wave 178 N=10:** framework − baseline = −4.24 pLDDT (N=10 records, 1 seed-equivalent).
- **Wave 179 3-seed × N=30:** framework − baseline = **−3.13 pLDDT** (3 paired samples,
  each a mean-of-30-records).

The 3-seed mean is **the same sign** as Wave 178 and a similar magnitude
(−3.13 vs −4.24). With three paired samples, every Δ is negative
(−5.79, −3.02, −0.60) — *not a single seed flipped*. The Δ shrinks as
seed index increases, suggesting seed=42 (highest-baseline) carries the
largest gap; this is consistent with the framework's restart-blend
budget being relatively stable across seeds while baseline pLDDT is more
seed-sensitive.

The paired t-test gives t=−2.089, p=0.172 — *not* significant at α=0.05
with df=2. This is a power issue (n=3), not a sign-flip issue. The
direction is robust: across 90 framework records (3 seeds × 30), the
mean pLDDT (51.66) is consistently below the 90-record baseline mean
(54.80).

**Verdict: insufficient_data_to_reject_noise, but signal direction
is consistent with Wave 178.** The framework pLDDT drop at kanzi
NFE=100 is *not* a fluke of one record — it persists across 3 seeds
and 90 records. To formally distinguish "real structural issue" from
"small-N noise under NFE=100", we would need either (a) N≥30 per seed
for several more seeds, or (b) a structural diagnostic of why NFE=100
specifically lands in this gap (e.g., does the framework's
NFE_REF=10 heuristic apply differently at NFE=100?). Wave 179 P5
should investigate the NFE=100-specific mechanism, not re-run more seeds.

The framework still wins **scPerplexity** at kanzi NFE=100 (Δ = −3.59,
p=0.024, framework consistently lower-better), so the structural
disagreement is real even if pLDDT is slightly worse — the framework's
self-consistency gain doesn't fully translate to folding confidence at
this NFE.

## 5. Significance discussion

### 5.1 Why n=3 paired samples is power-limited

A two-sided paired t-test with df=2 (n=3) has critical |t| = 4.303 at
α=0.05 and 9.925 at α=0.01. To reject the null at α=0.05 with 80%
power, the standardized effect size (Cohen's d = mean_diff / sd_diff)
must be ≈ 3.0. In our data:

| cell                | Δ pLDDT | sd_diff | Cohen's d |
|---------------------|--------:|--------:|----------:|
| lineageflow_nfe50   |   2.704 |   1.061 |      2.55 |
| lineageflow_nfe100  |   2.690 |   1.351 |      1.99 |
| lineageflow_nfe200  |   2.491 |   1.334 |      1.87 |
| kanzi_nfe50         |   0.680 |   1.504 |      0.45 |
| **kanzi_nfe100**    |  −3.135 |   1.500 |     −2.09 |
| kanzi_nfe200        |   2.342 |   1.786 |      1.31 |

Only lineageflow_nfe50 reaches the 80%-power threshold (d=2.55 > 3.0
*just below*; the framework is uniformly higher across seeds but
seed-to-seed spread is wide). All other cells are underpowered at
α=0.05, so the **non-significant p-values do not establish equivalence**
— they reflect limited sample size, not absence of effect.

### 5.2 Where p-values ARE meaningful

For **scPerplexity** the effect sizes are huge (Δ/SD ≈ 4–21 in absolute
value), so all 6 p-values are < 0.025 — framework *definitively*
improves self-consistency in every cell. This is the headline result.

### 5.3 framework_wins_both summary

| cell               | wins both? |
|--------------------|:----------:|
| lineageflow_nfe50  | ✓ |
| lineageflow_nfe100 | ✓ |
| lineageflow_nfe200 | ✓ |
| kanzi_nfe50        | ✓ |
| **kanzi_nfe100**   | **✗** |
| kanzi_nfe200       | ✓ |

## 8. Caveats

1. **n=3 paired samples is power-limited.** All pLDDT p-values except
   those reflecting |t| > 4.3 should be read as "direction is meaningful
   but we cannot claim statistical significance at α=0.05".
2. **Baseline is byte-stable across NFE** (per Wave 179 P2 §6.1), so
   the 3 baseline seeds per (model, nfe) are *not independent* across
   NFE — they are the same 3 records evaluated 3 times. The framework
   arm IS independent across NFE because the solver-budget differs.
   This means the **between-NFE comparisons are partially confounded
   with seed effects** for the baseline; the framework-vs-baseline
   paired test within each (model, nfe) is unaffected.
3. **Wall-min (32 min) is dominated by the eval re-run** (P3 had been
   killed at 12/36 cells; this session re-ran from cell 13 onward).
   The aggregation script itself runs in seconds.
